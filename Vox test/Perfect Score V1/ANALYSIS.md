# Why these shorts hold attention: analysis of the five references

The five references are *The Boy With The Empty Pot*, *Why You Always See A Trash Can At Disney*,
*He outsmarted the entire industry*, *Man who broke the bank at Monte Carlo* and *How the Eye In The Sky
works*. They were measured with these tools:

* **Transcripts:** Parakeet-TDT ASR, with word-level timings.
* **Stem split:** UVR, which separates the voice from the music+SFX bed.
* **Measurements:** loudness (EBU R128), pitch, tempo, key, onsets and cuts.

The reference image, "COMPLETION COMPULSION", gives the shape every script follows:

**HOOK → RISING ACTION → CONFLICT → COMEBACK (dip) → RISING ACTION → PAYOFF**

## 1. How each script maps onto the curve

| Beat | Eye in the Sky (79 s) | Monte Carlo (67 s) | Outsmarted the industry (68 s) | Empty Pot (61 s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–6: *"Imagine you have a magic pair of glasses… This is how fast it would take for you to get banned."* Premise plus a promised ending, which opens a loop. | 0–2.5: a question, *"Are all roulette numbers equally random?"* | 0–4: starts mid-action, *"He executed the code and it told him which horse would win."* | 0–10: a challenge plus stakes, *"…told them to grow the best plant… find an heir."* |
| **Rising** | 6–25: an escalation ladder. Nobody notices, then the table, then the dealer and pit boss, then the eye in the sky. | 2.5–33: observation, then the math, then the flaw, then the bets. *Disney* (30 s) is the compressed form of the same curve: Hook, then Rising, then Payoff, with no dip.

## 2. The techniques they share

1. **The first word lands at 0.0 s** in all five, over an action image. There is no intro and no logo.
2. **The open loop is stated in the first sentence.** Either it promises the ending (*"This is how fast…"*) or
   it asks a question the viewer needs answered.
3. **Re-hooks every 8–12 s.** These are pivot words that reset attention: *But… / However… /
   Nonetheless… / At this point… / And then he realized… / So…*
4. **An escalation ladder.** Each step raises the stakes or the authority involved, for example table, then pit
   boss, then surveillance, then management.
5. **Concrete specifics.** Numbers, names and objects: *$70,000, six assistants, 30 steps, 1 in 10
   million, a small scratch.*
6. **A dip before the end.** The hero loses, or is disbelieved, just before the turn.
7. **The payoff is a twist plus a short closer.** It is often funny, and it loops well into a rewatch.
8. **Short sentences delivered at speed:** 3.2–3.45 words/s on the low-poly channel and 2.7–2.9 on the
   storybook channel. Pauses are short (mean 0.57 s), except at dramatic stops.

**What this shows:**

* **Voice:** a deep, close-mic male read, dry with a tight room. Pitch movement is wide upward, which carries
  the emotion. Delivery is fast, with micro-pauses only at the beats.
* **Music:** a constant bed sitting 4–8 LU under the voice (low-poly channel). It is sparse in the hook, with
  bass and a held chord. Plucked or pizzicato ostinatos enter on the rising action. It drops out on the
  dramatic one-word lines and swells into the payoff. Key and tempo stay inside a minor key at 95–125 BPM.
* **SFX:** dense, short foley, roughly 30–50 events per minute, each lasting 0.1–0.25 s. Every on-screen
  action has a sound: footsteps, paper, impacts. Whooshes of 0.5–0.7 s sit on cuts and camera moves, with a
  low boom on reveals.

## 4. How *Perfect Score* uses this

The same concept as *Eye in the Sky* (an impossible winning streak, rising suspicion, a surprising
verdict) is moved to a school. Its moral is the honesty theme from *Empty Pot*: hard work beats
suspicion. Nothing in it involves gambling or anything haram.

| Beat | Time | Lines |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0.0–6.6 | *Imagine you scored a perfect hundred on every single exam. This is how fast it would take for your school to accuse you of cheating.* |
| Rising | 6.6–22.8 | nobody notices, then a good day, then the teacher notices, then silent watching, then the desk moved to the front, then *he's already watching* |
| Conflict | 22.8–42.7 | the hardest exam and an unsolved bonus, then *You solve it*, then the principal's office, the search, the camera replay and the comparison, then *Nothing. No notes, no signals, no copying.* |
| Comeback (dip) | 42.7–51.3 | *But nobody believes you*, then the parents are called, then the score is cancelled, then the school whispers, then **"Cheater."** |
| Rising | 51.3–64.4 | the principal's decision, then a new exam alone with three teachers watching, then 20 minutes, then the grading (three ticks) |
| Payoff | 64.4–81.0 | **"One hundred."**, then silence, then the reason (studying every night while others were on their phones), then an apology and the wall of honor, then the closer: *your desk gets moved again, to the very back. This time, so nobody can copy you.* |

**Delivery numbers:**

* **Pace:** 273 words in 80.2 s, which is 3.40 words/s (Eye: 3.45). The first word is at 0.0 s.
* **Re-hooks:** *But…* at 10.5 s, *Then comes…* at 22.8 s, *And now…* at 28.4 s, *But nobody…* at 42.8 s,
  *So the principal…* at 51.3 s, and *Because…* at 67.3 s.
* **Voice pitch:** a median of 130 Hz and a range of −6.4 / +8.8 semitones, close to the Eye narrator.
* **Mix:** −14.0 LUFS, with the voice 4.6 LU above the music+SFX bed (Eye: 4.7) and a true peak of −1 dBTP.
* **Score:** an original score in B minor at 120 BPM, built on the same curve:
  * sparse pulse and riser in the hook
  * pizzicato ostinato in the rising action
  * low staccato strings and timpani in the conflict
  * solo piano in the dip
  * an ostinato build and snare roll that stop dead before *"One hundred."*
  * an orchestral hit, then silence, then warm D major for the payoff
* **SFX:** about 187 synthesized foley events, around 140 per minute, plus room tone for each location. Every footstep sits on the walk
  cycle, every pen squeak follows the stroke being drawn, and the slam, stamp and bounce land on their
  contact frames.
