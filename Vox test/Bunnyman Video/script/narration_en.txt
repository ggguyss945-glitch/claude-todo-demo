That's the jingle from Pemberton's Department Store, Easter of 1987. It's the last thing my mother heard before she disappeared. She was seven years old. Three days later, she walked out of the woods. And she has been humming it ever since.

Every Easter Eve, my mother taped seven rules to our fridge. We followed every one of them. Every single rule was true. And not one of them was written to protect us from him.

I'm telling you this from a hospital. The doctors here are very kind. They just don't believe a word I say.

In 1987, at the Easter parade in Holloway, the Bunnyman rode the last float, handing out plastic eggs. Big grey suit. Black glass eyes. Buck teeth in a stitched-on smile. Inside it was a nineteen-year-old stock boy named Dale Whitlock.

There's a photo from that morning. A little girl in a yellow Easter dress, holding his paw. Laurie Price. My mom. He showed her a magic trick. Then he walked off into Holloway Woods. And she followed him.

The search dogs lost her scent at the old railroad bridge. Three days later, at sunrise on Easter morning, she walked out of the trees alone. Her dress was spotless. She was holding a golden plastic egg. And she was humming.
