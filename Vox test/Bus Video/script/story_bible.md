## Logline

On the night before her little brother's first day of school, 13-year-old Mina learns that the grinning cat mascot from a pulled 1994 bus-safety tape still drives a midnight route. He read her brother's name off a photo Mina posted. All night he beats Grandma Della's seven rules: he cuts the signal, traps Grandma in her own bus, copies voices, steals the family code word with Mina's own walkie-talkie and drives off with Sami. Mina wins Sami back at the last stop, but one boy has been on that bus since 1994. Nobody can call him home, because nobody ever asked his name.

## The message in one sentence

Knowing your name doesn't make someone safe, but learning someone's name can make them safe.

Safety lessons the story acts out: names on posts and backpacks can be read by anyone; a family code word is said by the grown-up first, never over a phone or walkie, and once it leaks it is dead; call 911 and tell a trusted adult; never get on any vehicle without your grown-up; "hurry, just you, our secret" is the alarm. Kindness lesson: sit with the one who sits alone, and ask their name. Respect lesson: the unsung bus driver ("Brakes, mirrors, names") is the hero, and Mina says sorry to her grandmother.

## Villain design spec: MISTER WHISTLESTOP

Origin: the mascot of "Ride Right with Mister Whistlestop!", a 1994 bus-safety tape made by Channel 6 in Larkfield and played in every school on a rolling TV cart. The tape ends with him asking each kid to WRITE their name, school and bus on a card and hold it up to the camera. That is how he reads names, then and now. The tape was pulled that fall, and nobody at Channel 6 remembers why.

### Silhouette and proportions
- About 8 ft tall and far too thin: legs about 55% of his height, a narrow torso, and arms that hang to his knees (and stretch further when he reaches: the arm down the bus aisle at 11:00).
- His head is about 1/7 of his body height, a mascot head sitting slightly crooked on the neck.
- The hat adds about 40% more height: a stovepipe far too tall, leaning about 12 degrees to his left, dented halfway up. This lean is the silhouette read. Indoors it is always crushed or folded against the ceiling.
- Readability test: in pure black against a lit bus door you see a crooked chimney hat, a long thin body and hands too long for the arms.

### Colors
- Hat: faded school-bus yellow (#D9A928, desaturated) with TWO black hazard bands that are always DIAGONAL, never horizontal (horizontal bands would bring back the striped stovepipe). Chewed, ragged brim and scuffs.
- Face mask fur: off-white (#EDE6D3), matted and yellowed at the edges (#C9B98A).
- Body fur (ears, back of the head, wrists, tail): dusty grey-brown tabby (#7A6A55 with darker #5A4C3D stripes). NEVER black: a black body with a white face and gloves is the Seuss scheme.
- Jacket: faded mustard bus-driver jacket (#A88A3A), sleeves too short so tabby fur shows at the wrists, an oval embroidered patch reading WHISTLESTOP.
- Bow tie: BLACK, with a small red STOP-sign octagon pin (#B3241E) in the center. The only red on him.
- Whistle: silver, on an orange lanyard (#E07A1F).
- Gloves: white cartoon gloves, slightly greyed.

### Face and grin
- Small black painted pupils, one a little higher than the other. No eyelids; they never blink.
- A ticket-punch hole through the left ear.
- THE GRIN: far too wide, past the cheeks, a flat row of white painted teeth, no tongue or inner mouth. It NEVER moves: not when he talks, not when he uses Grandma's, Mina's or Sami's voice. The narration plants this at 0:40 ("that never moves. Not even when he talks.").
- One exception: the defeat at the 3:00 bell, when the grin "doesn't fit his face" and slides out of register like a misaligned VHS layer.

### Gloves, props, tail
- Gloves have ONE KNUCKLE TOO MANY: four segments per finger that fold like hinges. Signature poses: fingers curling around a doorframe; fingers flat on both hallway walls; a glove "folded small" around Mina's fingers in the dark, gripping too tight.
- Mina's stolen walkie-talkie, pressed to the grin (rule 4 reveal).
- A clipboard roll call under one arm. It is a WATCHLIST: names from every bus in Larkfield, each line ending "Sat alone". Most names are crossed out (someone sat with them). Only Theo's never was. The last page has today's date and one name.
- A chrome ticket punch ("click-clack").
- Yellow tickets punched through the middle: SAMI. SEAT 4. LAST STOP 3:00 A.M.
- A thin tabby tail that hangs straight down and never moves.

### How he moves
- NEVER seen walking. He "tape-skips": every light flicker, blink or cut puts him closer, with VHS tracking lines and a 2-3 frame stutter. Fast moves get a horizontal tracking tear.
- He unfolds out of the bus door like a folding ladder, one segment at a time.
- Indoors he bends double, hat crushed against the ceiling.
- His head turns BACKWARDS (180 degrees) out of the driver's window in the road chase.
- When he leans toward someone, he hinges at the waist "down, and down" far past normal.

### Voice and sound
- A bright 1994 local-TV host voice on warbling tape, too cheerful, slightly out of sync, hiss underneath.
- He imitates Grandma, Mina and Sami perfectly. FX signature: when an imitation ends, the voice slides half a tape-speed down into his TV voice ("...Clever.").
- He kills phone signal near him ("No bars. Not since his headlights came."); bars return once he drives away. He can speak through any radio (the bus 9 dash radio at 9:15).
- Whistle: two short toots "fweet-fweet"; in the climax one long blast to drown out the calling. Ticket punch "click-clack". Air-brake hiss. The PSA jingle is instrumental, never with lyrics.

### ORIGINAL vs the Cat in the Hat (why this is safe)
The owner asked for a 1:1 copy of Dr. Seuss's Cat in the Hat. We did NOT copy it. It is a trademarked, copyrighted character whose owner enforces aggressively, and a horror copy risks a takedown and a channel strike. Mister Whistlestop shares only an ARCHETYPE (a tall cat in a top hat with white gloves and a bow tie, plus 1990s kids'-show creep), and archetypes are not protected.

| Red-and-white striped hat | Faded school-bus yellow with diagonal black hazard bands, battered and leaning |
| Red bow tie | Black bow tie with a STOP-sign pin |

| Rhyming anapestic verse | Short, unrhymed TV-host lines |

Hard limits (never break): no red-and-white stripes, no horizontal hat bands, no red bow tie, no black body fur, no rhyming verse, no fish, no two helper creatures, no umbrella or balancing tricks, no Seuss names, words or catchphrases. ## The 7 rules (verbatim, as in the narration)

Rule number one.
At midnight, turn off every light that faces the street. Take our name off anything he can see from the road. He can only call the names he can read.

Rule number two.
If he calls your name, never answer. Never say "here" to him. If one of us calls you, look first. Only answer a face you know.

Rule number three.
If anyone says I sent them, they have to say our word. You never say it first. Never say it where anyone else can hear.

Rule number four.
If the lights go out, find Sami and hold his hand. Don't let go until the lights come back.

Rule number five.
If he gets inside, lock yourselves in my room. Open the door only for the word.

Rule number six.
If he takes one of you, don't chase him on foot. Come get me. We meet him at his last stop.

Rule number seven.
Never get on his bus. Not for anybody. Not even for Sami.

Coda card (Mina's handwriting under Grandma's seven):
Rule number eight.
Sit with the one who sits alone. Ask their name.

Every rule is TRUE and is good real safety advice. He beats each one by adapting (he reads the post, fakes voices, traps the adult, cuts the signal, steals the word through a six-year-old, lures Mina to the window). The answer that wins was never on the door until Mina writes rule eight.

### His rules (story logic, kept consistent in the narration)
1. He can only call names he can READ: a mailbox, a backpack, a post, a 1994 Ride Right card. (He knows Della from 1994, Mina from her own post, Theo from Theo's card.)
2. A child who answers HIS roll call ("Here!") is his to lead away. A voice he fakes does not count ("Almost.").
3. A taken child is freed only at his LAST STOP: someone calls the child's name before the 3:00 bell and the child answers THEM. If the child answers him instead, the child stays. (So Mina cannot free Sami at the curb, and the two-Minas beat has stated stakes.)
4. At the bell, whoever is still on board is forgotten. Grown-ups stop seeing them; kids still can. Memories come back when the child is freed.
5. He keeps the kids nobody knows. Every morning he lets Theo ride his old bus, bus 9, as "one more chance" for someone to ask his name. Nobody ever has. He cannot imagine anyone ASKING a name: he only reads them. That is his blind spot, and why he taunts "Call him, then. You can't."
6. His clipboard is a watchlist of kids who sit alone. A name gets crossed out when someone sits with that kid.
7. Near him, phones lose signal; radios speak with his voice.

### The code word (repaired inside the story)
Grandma whispers the word ("LIGHTHOUSE"). He takes Mina's walkie in the blackout and, as "Mina", asks Sami to say the word. Mina's line: "Then it's not our word anymore. A word only works while it's secret." She tells Sami "The word's dead. Nobody opens." Sami still opens, because it is Grandma's voice and the word on the door, and he is six. The outro gives the real fix: the grown-up says it first; if it slips out, change it.
