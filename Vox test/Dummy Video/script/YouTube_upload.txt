DESCRIPTION
If you ever inherit an old ventriloquist dummy, don't laugh at its jokes. Don't put your hand inside it. And never, ever tell it goodnight.

My grandfather was the greatest ventriloquist who ever lived. Sixty years on stage, and nobody ever saw his lips move. The night after his funeral, we went to pack up his house. His dummy, Mister Winks, was waiting for us in his case, with a letter taped to the lid in Grandpa's shaky handwriting: seven rules for whoever was in the house that night.

I broke the fourth one. And one of the seven was a lie.
