[reading it carefully] Never open the hall closet at night. Never look in the walls alone.

[to the listener, ominous] Remember the hall switch that does nothing?

[lighter, amused] On Saturday, Mrs. Dabrowski from across the street brought us a pie. She was nice. She was nosy. She said.

[quiet, tense] That night at two, I stood in the upstairs hall, and I flipped the switch that does nothing.

[slow, creeping dread] Somewhere above me, in a place that was sealed, a light came on. A thin yellow line, around the edge of the hatch, inside the hall closet.

[terrified, quiet] And a voice in the ceiling, right above my head, whispered,

[tempted, tense] I wanted to open that closet so badly. Rule four. Not alone. Not at night.

[firm] I didn't open it.

[controlled, steady] I flipped the switch off. I walked, I did not run, to my parents' room, and I told my dad everything.

Dad sighed.

[firm, deliberate] Rule number five.

[reading it carefully] Know your address. Sing it.

[fond, a little exasperated] Theo had a song. He sang it fifty times a day. In the bath. At dinner. In his sleep. It drove us crazy.

[quiet, uneasy] That night, I heard it through the wall by my bed.

[chilled] But not in Theo's voice.

[low, disturbed] In a man's voice. Slow. Low. Wrong.

[tense, building] Then the knocking started. And the voice in the wall started talking. And it said things I had heard before.

[slow realization] It wasn't Dad's voice.

[stunned] They were Dad's words.

[dark realization, faster] No. They were the note's words. Every excuse my dad had given us, all summer, was written down for him on day one. By the man who wanted us to have an excuse for every sign he left.

[firm, deliberate] Rule number six.

[reading it carefully] If someone is inside, don't look for him. Get away from him, get behind a locked door with a phone, and call.
