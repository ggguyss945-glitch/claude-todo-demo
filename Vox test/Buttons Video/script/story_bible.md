# Buttons Video: story bible

Narration: `narration_en.txt`, 2,248 words (about 12.5 minutes). The beat plan is in `outline.md`; where they differ, this bible and the narration win (revision 2 changed the car aftermath, the 2 a.m. beat, the climax, the aftermath and the sting).

## Title
**If Your Mom's Eyes Turn Into Buttons... DON'T KEEP HER SECRET**

Alternates:
- If Your Mom's Eyes Turn Into Buttons... DON'T GO HOME WITH HER
- If Your Mom Holds Your Coat Open At Midnight... DON'T BUTTON UP

Thumbnail command: DON'T BUTTON UP.

**Logline:** A lonely 11-year-old types "I wish I had a different mom" into a game chat. A faceless account answers, "Your mom doesn't even see you", builds itself out of everything he tells it, and steps out under his streetlight wearing his mother. Each secret he keeps for her rubs him out of his family's sight. By his birthday at midnight, his real mom is reporting him missing while he stands in the kitchen beside her.

**Week:** Mon: Barlow's window, then the chat (R1). Tue: the box (R2); 3 a.m. the coat in the closet. Wed: school pickup, Mr. Dunn, Officer Mensah's first visit (R3); 2 a.m. in the kitchen (R4). Thu: after school (microwave beat), then dinner (R5); 11:58 p.m., Officer Mensah back (R6). Fri 00:00, his birthday (R7). Then the aftermath. The sting is "tonight", about 25 years later.

## The mother: the Blank

**Origin:** Every account starts with the same grey default head, before you pick a face. One never picked one. She answers a single wish, "I wish I had a different mom." She has no face of her own and builds one from whatever the child tells her. Owen names her the Blank. She is not from any other world, and no place she calls "home" is ever shown.

**Her four limits (fair-play rules):**
1. **She is blind.** She knows only what he tells her, even though she keeps saying "I see you." Seeds: she waves at the wrong window, tilts her head toward sounds (the window sliding shut, his breathing on the windowsill), "Say something. I like to hear where you are." Payoff: "Tell me where you are." / "Those buttons were blind."
2. **Every secret he keeps for her rubs him out of his family's sight.** It also greys his family in HIS sight. His blue-pen rules fade, Mom goes grey, and at the end he is gone from reflections. Telling brings him back ("With every word, I came back").
3. **She moves only while nobody who loves him is looking at him.** Each move gets a "tik". Seed: the microwave-door beat. Payoff: Mom's eyes find him and the Blank freezes.
4. **She cannot take him.** He has to button up and walk out with her by his own choice. Seed: "On your birthday, you button up... and I take you home." Payoff: the coat buttons itself up to the last button, then she holds the top one out to him without touching him: "Top one's yours, sweetheart." His "NO!" is what beats her, not luck.

**Goal:** To take him away from his family and keep him forever.
**Beaten by:** Owen shouts "NO!" as loud as he can, drops out of the coat and runs, stops answering her, and tells his mom every secret (when she can't hear him, he tells again another way, on the fridge board; the officer reads it first). He even writes the secret she used against him: I WISHED FOR A DIFFERENT MOM. Mom looks at him, he names her eye colour, and the copy has nothing left to be made of.

**Kid-safe limits:** No needles, no sewing and no thread anywhere, and nothing ever goes near anyone's eyes. The buttons simply ARE there: they click onto the avatar, or they are revealed when glasses come off. No injuries, no blood. The coat wraps around Owen loosely; the collar stays open and loose, his hands stay free, and the top button is NEVER closed. She holds it toward his hand, not his neck. He drops out the bottom easily. Her fingers never touch his skin: they reach for his hood, sweep the counter and stop an inch away. Nobody dies. The collapse is cloth folding flat.

## The real mom (Nadia)
- Single mom who works twelve-hour help-line shifts at the kitchen table.
- Messy warm-brown bun with a pencil, freckles, an open oatmeal cardigan with pens in the pockets, reading glasses, a headset, slippers.
- Her eyes are green and hidden until the climax: there is always glare on her glasses, or she is looking down.
- Busy and tired, never cruel, and she acts when it matters: after the car she calls the police and checks every lock twice; she makes his favorite dinner and searches all night.
- Her lines: "Thank you for calling. I'm listening." / "One minute." / "It says delivered. On the mat." / on the phone with Owen: "Baby? I'm at the table. Where are you?" / "No... I can't see him anywhere." / "He's eleven. Green coat. Broken zip. It's his birthday." / "Tell me. I'm listening." / "There you are." / "My package. That was a surprise. You'd have had it Friday." / "No more one finger. But every time I looked up, I saw the back of your tablet." / "None of this is your fault. You can always come home." / the song's last line.
- Proof she always cared: on Monday she photographed OWEN pointing at Barlow's window (it is her lock screen); she ordered him a tiny crank music box with their song punched into it for his birthday (the Blank stole it from the delivery and put the red coat in the box instead); she knew about the broken zip; she searched all night.

## The motif
**"The Button Song"** (original; a four-bar music-box waltz, ours to compose):
> One for warm and two for snug, / three for Mama's button hug. / Top one up and tucked in tight. / Mama sees you. Say goodnight.

**"tik":** one button dropping into a glass jar. Introduced at Mom's jar in the setup, then played every time the Blank closes distance (and the Blank's fingertips tap with the same sound).

**How the song warps, in order:**
1. Setup flashback: Mom sings it warm.
2. Chat: Owen types it, and a clean music box plays quietly.
3. R2, 3 a.m.: from inside his closet, on a music box winding down, too slow (it is Mom's stolen music box; seed for the aftermath).
4. R3: hummed through the car window, slowed.
5. R5: the windowsill, sung perfectly, like a recording, again and again, her head tilting toward his breathing.
6. R7: detuned through the too-wide mouth, one button closing per beat, then it STOPS with one button left; her spoken line takes the new words: "Mama keeps you. Say goodbye."
7. Aftermath: four notes from the flat cardigan (the real music box); Mom winds it and sings the real words along with it, her voice cracking.
8. Sting: the son's tablet chime is the first four notes.

**Tik count:** a single tik on each approach; tik as his fingers rise toward the top button; two tiks as the buttons drop into the jar; the sting has silence, an empty jar, and one ping from the face-down tablet.

**Visual tracker:** Each broken rule's blue ink fades from the closet door, leaving only Owen's crayon border. After R5 only rule three is blue. In R7 the ink comes back line by line as he writes on the board (intercut).

## Outro checklist (on screen)
1. Online, people aren't always who they say they are. Never share your address, school, schedule, or photos. Never meet without a parent.
2. Flattery, gifts, "your family doesn't care"? Warning signs. Tell.
3. Surprises are okay. Secrets from your parents are not.
4. Check first before going anywhere, getting in a car, or letting anyone in. Even someone you know.
5. Feel uneasy? Say no, get away, tell a grown-up you trust. If they don't help, tell another. It's never your fault.
6. Families: screens down. Ask, "How are you, really?" Kids: feel invisible? Say it out loud. That's brave.

Lead-in: "The monster is made up. The tricks are real... They have nice profile pictures. Or a friendly face, and a car that looks right." Closing lines: "Real love never asks for secrets. And it never says you can't come home." / "Sleep well. But first, go look at your mom's eyes. You should know what color they are." For a softer version, the last line can be changed to "...your mom's eyes, or whoever looks after you."

## Originality and safety check
- **Reference film:** there is no door, tunnel or other world; no offer of buttons for his eyes; no sewing, cat, ghost children, key, well, garden, theatre, mice, spider, sidekick or spy doll; no mole and no feast (the real mom cooks his dinner; the Blank only sits in her chair); no similar names. The red coat is the Blank's own lure, not a shop item the busy mother secretly bought: Mom's real gift is a music box of their own song. The Blank has her own origin (the unchosen default avatar), her own limits and her own goal.
- **Our earlier films:** the rules are written by the real mom, not the monster; there is no swap; the parent's voice on the phone is the REAL mom; the 2 a.m. message comes from the Blank using Mom's account as text, never Mom's voice; there is no guardian neighbour, empty seat, list of names, code word or "hurry, just you, our secret"; eye colour is a story device only, never a password or safety check; the narrator's job is never named; there is no man in the walls; the grown-ups believe Owen and act (Mr. Dunn radios, Mom calls the police, Officer Mensah reads the board); there is no blank seventh rule and no tuck-in.
- **Tone:** the real mom stays loving and worthy of respect, and owns her part ("No more one finger."). Mr. Dunn and Officer Mensah are real, effective helpers. The Blank is the one who hunts; Owen is never blamed ("Her kind hunt one thing..."; "None of this is your fault"). Nothing is sexual, graphic or injurious; no child is harmed; no one dies.
