# Buttons Video: beat outline

## Title
**If Your Mom's Eyes Turn Into Buttons... DON'T KEEP HER SECRET**

Alternates:
- If Your Mom's Eyes Turn Into Buttons... DON'T GO HOME WITH HER
- If Your Mom Holds Your Coat Open At Midnight... DON'T BUTTON UP

Thumbnail command: DON'T BUTTON UP

## Judging

| Concept | Creep/tension | Twists | Lesson woven in | Originality | Showability | Safety/respect | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: Owen / chat thing | 9 | 9 | 9 | 8 | 8 | 9 | **52** |
| B: Frame Lady / Adam | 8 | 8 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 47 |
| C: The Sample / Eli | 8 | 9 | 7 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 48 |

**Winner: A.** It has the most real-world origin (a lonely kid types his wish to strangers online), and every rule is a grooming step that the villain actually acts out. It also has the strongest gut-punch (the narrator has become the parent on the headset). B and C both use a stock photo as the origin, and B's "come into the picture" drifts toward the reference film's other world. C's rule one ("nobody is allowed to be nicer to you than me") sounds possessive coming from a real mom.

**Fixes to A:**
- The villain is renamed from "Better Mom" to **the Blank**. "Better Mom" follows the same pattern as the reference film's villain name.
- The felt avatar is replaced with the grey default profile head, so nothing echoes the reference film's doll.
- Mom's mole is cut, because the reference character has one.
- The 2 a.m. pancake feast and the beat where she cuts up his food are cut, because they are too close to the reference film's dinner scenes. At 2 a.m. she now gathers information instead, which is the real grooming step.
- The song no longer plays on a car radio, to avoid our Ice Cream device.
- The card no longer says "our secret", to avoid the Bus phrasing.
- The tuck-in beat is cut, because Walls already used it.
- Her limits are cut down to four clean ones.
- Everything is compressed into one week, with a midnight deadline on his birthday.

**Grafts:**
- From C: the silent word switch in the narration. After rule five the narrator calls the real mom "the headset lady", and this lands at rule six.
- From C: "everything she gave me was already mine".
- From C: Mom steps between him and the monster and looks only at him.
- From B: Mom's glasses catch the laptop screen as two black shiny circles, so for one second the REAL mom has button eyes. This puts the title image on the real mom.
- From B, as a mutual twist: "Every time I looked up, all I saw was the back of your tablet."

## The villain: the Blank

**Origin:** Every account starts with the same grey default head, before you pick a face. One of them never picked a face. It answers a single wish: *"I wish I had a different mom."* Kids used to whisper that into their pillows. Now they type it to strangers, and she is always the first to answer. She has no face of her own. She builds one out of whatever the child tells her.

**Her four limits:**
1. She knows only what he tells her. She is blind, even though she says "I see you" all the time.
2. Every secret he keeps for her makes her more real and wipes him a little more out of his family's sight. He greys, then turns see-through, then is gone from reflections. It also greys his family in HIS sight.
3. She moves closer only while nobody who loves him is looking at him. Each move is marked by a "tik".
4. She cannot take him. He has to walk out with her of his own choice, buttoned into her coat.

**Goal:** To take him away from his family and keep him forever. Her line: "You'll never have to wait for anyone again." Her deadline is midnight on his birthday: "On your birthday, I'll take you home." Where "home" is never shown. There is no other world.

**She is defeated** by the real-world moves: he says no, gets away, tells every secret, and keeps telling. Then she runs out of things to be made of. Mom looks at him (so she freezes), and he finally knows his mom's eye colour. Buttons are what you put where you never looked.

## The mother and her forms

All forms are our own design, built on the real mom's silhouette.

**Real Mom (Nadia):**
- Messy bun with a pencil stuck through it, freckles across her nose.
- An oatmeal cardigan, open and rumpled, with pockets full of pens.
- Reading glasses that always catch screen glare, a help-line headset, slippers.
- Her eyes are green, but we NEVER see them until the climax: there is always glare on her glasses, or she is looking down.

**Form 0, the chat avatar:** A grey default profile head. It fills in detail by detail as Owen types (setup).

**Form 1, "normal":** The real mom. The Blank can fake this form only with glass over her eyes: sunglasses in rule 3, Mom's reading glasses in rule 4.

**Form 2, button-eyed and sweet:**
- Mom's face and clothes, made too neat: a glossy, perfect bun and slightly too-smooth skin with a grey undertone.
- The cardigan is buttoned to the throat with shiny black buttons that match her eyes.
- Her eyes are two shiny black four-hole buttons with a white glint. She never blinks.
- Her smile is a little too big.
- When she turns fast, for one frame her face is the blank grey default head.
- She is a little taller every time we see her.
- Appears in: the cold open; the end of setup (the buttons click onto the avatar); the streetlight and lobby glass (R1); the car once the sunglasses come down (R3); the 2 a.m. table and the microwave reflection (R4); dinner, where her head is at the lamp (R5).

**Form 3, creepy:**
- As tall as the ceiling and very thin, with her neck bending along the ceiling.
- The cardigan is stretched into a long tube, and its button row gets longer.
- Long pointed fingers that click like buttons, and a smile far too wide.
- The black button eyes stay.
- Appears in: a glimpse in R3 (her arm stretches out of the car window, joints bending wrong); R5 (she stands and her head sets the lamp swinging); in full in R6 (unfolding from behind the fridge) and R7; then she collapses into an empty, flat cardigan.

**Hard rules:** No needles, no sewing, and nothing near anyone's eyes. The buttons simply ARE there, or they show when her glasses come off. No injuries. The top button sits under his chin, with no pressure on his throat.

## The real mom

Nadia is a single mom who works twelve-hour help-line shifts at the kitchen table.
- On calls she says, "Thank you for calling. I'm listening."
- To Owen she raises one finger: "One minute."
- She eats standing up and hasn't sung their song in years.
- At Barlow's window she "didn't even look", but she lifted her phone. She was photographing the coat to save up for his birthday.
- She noticed his broken zip.
- Her lock screen is his face.

When the secrets wipe him out, she searches all night. At dinner she doesn't touch her plate, she calls everyone, she waits on the couch facing the front door with her shoes on, and she calls the police. The moment she can reach him, she pulls off the headset and her glasses ("Tell me. I'm listening."), steps between him and the thing, and says: "None of this is your fault. You can always come home."

Tired and busy, never cruel.

## The motif

**"The Button Song":** Mom's rhyme for buttoning his coat. It is a four-bar music-box waltz (ours to compose):
> One for warm and two for snug,
> three for Mama's button hug.
> Top one up and tucked in tight.
> Mama sees you. Say goodnight.

**"tik":** A single button dropping into a glass jar. It plays every time the Blank gets closer.

**How the song warps, in order:**
1. Flashback: Mom sings it warm.
2. Owen types it into the chat; a clean music box plays quietly.
3. R2: the melody is hummed slowly inside his closet, with no words.
4. R3: the Blank hums it through the car window, slowed.
5. R5 night: she sings it perfectly, too perfectly, like a recording.
6. R7: it comes out detuned through her too-wide mouth, with one button clicking shut per line. The words have changed: "Top one up and buttoned tight. Mama keeps you. Say goodbye."
7. Aftermath: real Mom sings it right, her voice cracking.
8. Sting: the son's tablet uses the song's first four notes as its notification chime.

**How the tik plays:** a tik on each approach; two tiks as the buttons drop into Mom's jar; then in the sting, silence and an empty jar.

**Visual tracker:** Each broken rule fades from the closet door, Mom's blue pen first, until only Owen's crayon is left. In the aftermath, all seven lines come back.

## The seven rules (verbatim)

1. Never tell anyone online where we live. Not even with a picture.
2. If someone gives you a present and says don't tell Mom... tell Mom.
3. Check with Mom before you go anywhere, get in any car, or open the door. Even for somebody you know.
4. Surprises are okay. Secrets are not.
5. If someone says mean things about our family, they're not your friend.
6. If you feel sad or invisible, say it out loud. Even if I'm busy. Especially then.
7. If anyone tries to take you away from us: say no, get away, and tell. Then keep telling.

Owen breaks 1, 2, 4 and 5. He keeps 3, keeps 6 (late), and keeps 7.

## Beat outline

### COLD OPEN (about 60 words)
Black screen. The music box plays four notes.

- **"If someone online asks what color your mom's eyes are... don't answer."** On screen: a tablet glows in a dark bedroom with the chat bubble "what color are her eyes?" and a blinking cursor.
- **"If a present comes with a card that says don't tell your mom... don't hide it."** On screen: a red coat in a box; a card; a boy's hands pushing it into a closet.
- **"And if your mom holds your coat open at midnight, with two shiny black buttons where her eyes should be, and whispers... 'Button up, sweetheart. We're going home'... don't."** On screen: the kitchen, and the Blank (form 2) holding the red coat open. Tik.
- **"Your real mom is standing right behind you. She just can't see you anymore."** On screen: we pull back. The real Mom on her headset looks straight through a grey, see-through boy. Cut.

Seeds T5, T7.

### HOOK (about 75 words)
On screen: the closet door with the Brave List (blue pen, crayon border), seven lines.

- "When I was eight, my mom and I wrote seven rules on the inside of my closet door. Her words in blue pen, my crayon all around them. We called it the Brave List. When I was eleven, I broke four of them in one week."
- Promise: "By midnight on my birthday, there was a police officer in our kitchen, my mom was crying my name... and I was standing right next to her."
- "I still have the buttons. In a jar on my desk. Next to my headset."

On screen: an adult desk with a glass jar holding two black four-hole buttons beside a headset. No adult is drawn.

Seeds T9.

### SETUP (about 260 words)

**S1, the kitchen.**
- Mom at the table on her headset: "Thank you for calling. I'm listening."
- Owen starts to talk; she raises one finger: "One minute."
- Narration: "Strangers got 'I'm listening.' I got one finger."
- Mom eats standing up. Owen eats with his tablet propped on the milk carton (seeds T8).
- Glare on her glasses: we never see her eyes (seeds T7).
- She drops a loose button into the jar on the windowsill: tik (the sound is introduced).
- The fridge whiteboard reads FRI: O'S B-DAY.

**S2, Barlow's window.**
- Owen's green coat flaps open: the broken zip.
- In the window is a red coat with seven black buttons. He points.
- Mom, on a call, doesn't turn her head, but she lifts her phone (seeds T3).
- Narration: "She didn't even look."

**S3, flashback in warm colours.**
- Younger Mom buttons little Owen's coat and sings the Button Song in full.
- Narration: "She hadn't sung it in years. My coat had a zip now. A broken one."

**S4, Monday night, the chat.**
- He types: I WISH I HAD A DIFFERENT MOM. Ping.
- A grey default head: no name, no picture.
- The Blank: "I can be her. Tell me about her."
- He does. Her bun appears on the grey head, then the pencil, the freckles, the cardigan. He tells it about Saturday pancakes, and he types the Button Song.
- The Blank: "What color are her eyes?" A long beat.
- Owen: "I don't know."
- Two shiny black buttons click onto the grey face. Tik.
- The Blank: "That's okay. I'll see you for both of us."
- Narration: she never picked a name; she waited for me to give her one. "I called her the Blank."
- Narration: "She didn't find me because I was special. She found me because I told strangers I was invisible."

Seeds T1, T2, T6, T7. Lesson: point 6.

### RULE 1 (about 190 words)
**"Rule number one. Never tell anyone online where we live. Not even with a picture."**

- In the chat, the Blank: "You're so grown-up. Send me your view, so I can picture you."
- He photographs the view from his window: the streetlight, the bus-stop sign, the corner of Barlow's. He sends it.
- Close-up: on the closet door, rule one's blue ink fades.
- That night, under that exact streetlight, a woman in Mom's cardigan, buttoned to the throat, looks up and waves. Headlights sweep across her face and two black buttons flash. She is waving at the window NEXT to his (seeds T6).
- Ping: "I see you." He ducks. Tik.
- He looks again: she is at the lobby's glass door with her palms flat on the glass, her head tilting toward the sound of his window sliding shut. Tik.
- He looks again: she's gone.
- Ping: "Goodnight, sweetheart. Button up."

**Lands T1:** "She wasn't far away. She was made of everything I'd told her. And she was standing outside, wearing my mom."

**Lesson:** points 4 and 1. A photo shows where you live, and flattery is how they get in.

### COMMENT PROMPT (about 50 words)
"Okay. Before rule two, one word, in the comments. If a mom who ALWAYS sees you, never busy, never 'one minute', showed up tonight... would you pick her? Buttons, or Mom? One word."

### RULE 2 (about 170 words)
**"Rule number two. If someone gives you a present and says don't tell Mom... tell Mom."**

- A box on the doormat; the label has Mom's name on it (seeds T3).
- Inside is the red coat from Barlow's, with seven black buttons.
- The card, in neat looping writing: "Because she never even looked. Don't tell her."
- He hides it in his closet.
- From the kitchen, Mom into her headset: "It says delivered. On the mat." He thinks it's about a caller's order (seeds T3).
- Morning: rule two's blue ink is gone, and only his crayon is left. Mom at the table is a shade greyer.
- 3 a.m.: inside his closet, click... click... like someone counting. Then the Button Song melody, hummed slowly.
- He opens the door. The red coat hangs buttoned to the very top, filled out as if someone is wearing it. Then it slowly sags empty (seeds the R7 image). Tik.

**Lesson:** points 1 and 2. A gift plus "don't tell" means you tell.

### RULE 3 (about 190 words)
**"Rule number three. Check with Mom before you go anywhere, get in any car, or open the door. Even for somebody you know."**

- Wednesday pickup in the snow: Mom's car, with Mom's dent in the bumper.
- Mom at the wheel in sunglasses. Through the open window she hums the Button Song, slowed.
- The Blank: "Get in, sweetheart. I got off early. Pancakes?"
- Narration: "My mom never got off early. And I'd told only one person about pancakes."
- He steps back and calls Mom. Real Mom, at home: "Baby? I'm at the table. Where are you?"
- The driver lowers her sunglasses: buttons.
- Her arm slides out of the window, longer and longer, the joints bending wrong, her fingers clicking at his hood (the first glimpse of form 3).
- He runs to Mr. Dunn. Mr. Dunn: "You did right. You checked. Stand with me."
- When they look, only tire tracks are left in the snow.
- Mom arrives and hugs him hard. He tells her about the car. Not about the chat.
- Ping: "Tell her about me, and she'll know you wished for a different mom. Your fault."
- Rule three stays in blue ink.

**Lands T2.**

**Lesson:** point 3 (check first, even with a face you know); points 1 and 5 ("your fault" is a trick; telling half is not telling).

### RULE 4 (about 165 words)
**"Rule number four. Surprises are okay. Secrets are not."**

- 2 a.m. His tablet pings: "Come to the kitchen."
- "Mom" sits at Mom's laptop with her back to him, in reading glasses. His tablet pings with every key she presses. She is the one typing.
- She turns and takes the glasses off. Click. Buttons.
- The Blank: "There you are. Sit. Say something. I like to hear where you are." (seeds T6)
- She asks everything: which nights Mom sleeps deepest, when the bus comes, which door is nearest the stairs.
- The Blank: "It's not a secret. It's a surprise. Your mom finds out when we're gone. On your birthday, I'll take you home."
- He nods. He keeps it. Rule four fades.
- Thursday, after school: in the black microwave door, Mom looks down at her screen, and the Blank is standing behind Owen. Mom looks up: gone. She looks down: closer. Tik. Up, then down: her chin is almost on his shoulder. Tik.

**Lesson:** point 2 (a surprise is shared soon; a forever secret is a warning sign); point 4 (schedules); point 6 (looking away leaves the gap).

### RULE 5 (about 165 words)
**"Rule number five. If someone says mean things about our family, they're not your friend."**

- Thursday dinner. Mom stands at the window on the headset, her plate untouched. Owen's place is set with his favourite, and she keeps glancing at the door. Owen sits there with his tablet. Narration: "She didn't even sit down." (seeds T5)
- The Blank, taller now with her bun brushing the lamp, sits in Mom's chair with her chin on her long folded fingers.
- The Blank: "Watch. She doesn't even see you."
- He waves. Mom turns and looks straight through him. Her glasses catch the laptop: two black shiny circles. Narration: "For one second, I couldn't tell my mothers apart." (graft from B; seeds T7 and T8)
- Mom, into the headset: "No... I can't see him anywhere." (seeds T5)
- The Blank: "Only I see you."
- Owen: "Only you."
- Every line in Mom's pen fades off the closet door except rule three. Mom turns pencil-grey.
- The Blank stands, and her head sets the lamp swinging.
- The switch, grafted from C: "After dinner, Mom sat on the end of my bed and sang our song. Every word right. Out in the kitchen, the headset lady was still on the phone." (seeds T4)

**Lesson:** point 1. Someone who talks down your family is pulling you away, and they make the distance they point at.

### RULE 6 (about 170 words)
**"Rule number six. If you feel sad or invisible, say it out loud. Even if I'm busy. Especially then."**

- 11:58 p.m. The headset lady is on the couch facing the front door, with her shoes on.
- Officer Mensah stands in the kitchen with a notebook.
- Mom, crying: "He's eleven. Green coat. Broken zip. It's his birthday."
- Owen is right there. The microwave door reflects Mom and the officer, and no Owen.

**Lands T5:** "She wasn't ignoring me. She couldn't see me. Every secret I kept had rubbed me out, line by line, like her pen on my door. All night, the world thought I was missing. I was standing in my own kitchen."

**Lands T4:** "The headset lady. Listen to that. Three days, and I'd stopped calling my own mother Mom."

- He keeps rule six. Owen: "Mom. I'm here. I feel invisible."
- She turns her head, as if she heard a whisper.
- The whiteboard clock ticks to midnight: FRI.
- Behind the fridge, the Blank unfolds in full form 3, up to the ceiling, holding the red coat open.
- The Blank, voice wrong: "Happy birthday. She can't hear you. Only me. Button up. We're going home."

**Lesson:** point 6. Kids, say it out loud; families, look up.

### RULE 7 and CLIMAX (about 240 words)
**"Rule number seven. If anyone tries to take you away from us: say no, get away, and tell. Then keep telling."**

- Her long fingers wrap the coat around him. Each button clicks shut on its own, one per line of the detuned song: "...top one up and buttoned tight. Mama keeps you. Say goodbye."
- The top button sits under his chin.
- Owen: "No."
- He goes silent, drops down and out of the bottom of the coat. She is left holding a coat buttoned to the top with nobody in it (pays off R2).
- She turns her head toward where his voice was. The Blank: "Tell me where you are."

**Lands T6:** "She had never seen me. Not once. She only knew what I told her. So I stopped telling her. And I told my mom."

- He grabs the whiteboard marker. Under FRI he writes: MOM IT'S OWEN I'M HERE.
- The marker squeaks. The Blank lunges at the sound, her fingers sweeping the counter.
- Officer Mensah: "Ma'am. The board."
- Mom pulls off her headset AND her glasses: "Tell me. I'm listening."
- He writes every secret: THE CHAT. THE PHOTO. THE COAT. THE CAR. 2 AM. ONLY YOU. With each one, colour floods back into him like a photo developing, and the closet-door ink returns line by line (intercut).
- The long fingers are an inch from his hood. Mom's eyes find him: "There you are."
- The Blank freezes mid-reach (limit 3).
- Mom steps between them, pulls him in, and turns her back on the Blank. She looks only at him (graft from C).
- Owen looks into his mother's eyes: real, tired, wet. "Green."

**Lands T7:** "Buttons are what you put where you never looked."

- The Blank folds in on herself like an empty coat, down to a flat cardigan on the tiles. Two black buttons roll to Mom's slippers.

**Lesson:** points 5 and 7. Say no, get away, tell, and keep telling. It is never your fault.

### AFTERMATH (about 110 words)
- Mom drops both buttons into her jar: tik, tik. She screws the lid on.
- She sees the red coat. Mom: "My package came. That was your birthday coat."

**Lands T3.** Her lock screen is Owen's face. The Barlow's photo is in her gallery.

- Narration (graft from C): "Everything she ever gave me was already mine. The coat. The pancakes. Even the song."
- Mom: "Every time I looked up, all I saw was the back of your tablet."

**Lands T8:** "She was right. I didn't know her eyes, because I'd been looking at a screen too."

- Mom: "None of this is your fault. You can always come home."
- She buttons the red coat on him and sings the Button Song right, her voice cracking: "Mama sees you. Say goodnight."
- With no narration, the two of them rewrite the Brave List together in fresh pen.

### STING / GUT-PUNCH (about 120 words)
- "I'm grown up now. I have a son. He's ten. I work from home. Headset on, all day. Just like her." Never say what the job is.
- On screen: the desk, the headset, the jar. The lid is off. The jar is empty. Silence, no tik.
- The son's room: the tablet glows and chimes the song's first four notes. A grey default head. The chat reads: "I could be your dad. What color are his eyes?" His son has typed: "I don't know."

**Lands T9.**

- "I set the headset down." On screen: adult hands lower the headset; the door opens; the boy looks up.
- "Look at me. How are you, really?" (the narrator's own voice)
- "And he told me everything." The son looks at him. Son: "Brown. Your eyes are brown."
- On the tablet, the grey head goes still, still waiting. Cut.

**Lesson:** point 6 for families. The danger comes back every generation, and looking up beats it.

### OUTRO (about 240 words)
- "Okay. Breathe. You can turn a light on now. None of this happened. There's no Blank, no Barlow's, no red coat with seven black buttons. Owen and his mom were made up by someone who looked up from a laptop at midnight... and couldn't remember the last time he'd really looked at his own family."
- "The monster is made up. Every trick she used is real. People who want to take a kid away from his family don't have button eyes. They have nice profile pictures."
- "So here's the real list." The checklist is shown on screen (below).
- "Real love never asks for secrets. And it never says you can't come home."
- The comment payoff (below).
- Like: "If this story made you look up from your screen, leave a like, so I know you did."
- Subscribe: "And subscribe. On Night Policy, the next story comes to you... and it will never ask you to keep it a secret. Show it to your mom."
- Last line: "Sleep well. But first, go look at your mom's eyes. You should know what color they are." A softer option: "...your mom's eyes, or whoever tucks you in."

No "Next time" teaser.

## Comment prompt and payoff
**Prompt (after R1):** "Buttons, or Mom? One word."

**Payoff:** "Did you notice what I just did? I got you to tell a whole internet of strangers how you feel about your mom. That's exactly how she found me. So the real answer doesn't go in the comments. Say it to your mom's face tonight. Then come back and reply under your own comment: Told her."

## Outro checklist (on screen, 6 items)
1. Someone online asks where you live, your school, your schedule, or for a photo? Don't send it. Never meet them without your parent.
2. Flattery, gifts, "your family doesn't care"? Warning signs. Tell.
3. Surprises are okay. Secrets from your parents are not.
4. Check first before you go anywhere, get in any car, or let anyone in. Even if they seem to know your family.
5. Feel uneasy? Say no, get away, tell a grown-up you trust. If they don't help, tell another. It's never your fault.
6. Families: screens down. Ask "How are you, really?" Kids: if you feel invisible, say it out loud.
