## Logline

It is the coldest night in Larkfield since 1958. Twelve-year-old Adam is home alone with his six-year-old sister Lina, and he follows the seven rules that grumpy old Mr. Kessler tapes to every door on Birch Lane. A smiling pink-and-white ice cream truck plays its song street by street, then parks outside. All night the ice cream man hunts them: a free cone that gets inside and kills their heat, their father's voice on the radio, Lina lured out to his window, a transformation into a spider-legged skull, their mother's voice at the door, legs through the attic hatch. The whole time, the old man across the street looks like the villain's accomplice. Then Adam realises who the hunt was really for. Every scare kept every house inside, so that nobody would cross the street to the old man who wrote the rules.

## The message in one sentence

Cold, like every predator, takes whoever is alone, so the bravest rule is the one you keep together: check on the neighbour nobody checks on.

Lessons the story ACTS OUT (named only in the short outro):
1. **Lures:** "free", "just come to the window", a familiar voice that says the opposite of what your parent said, "sleep is warm". Nothing a stranger gives is free. Don't open the door, don't bring it in, tell a grown-up.
2. **Hypothermia signs and first aid:** stopping shivering, sleepiness, feeling warm, mittens off, wandering out. Adam tries to call for help first (the radio is jammed), then dry socks, layers, a blanket around her middle, body heat inside his coat, and he stays with her. Shivering coming back is a good sign. Outro: call emergency services (not "911": the audience is international).
3. **Frostbite:** white, waxy fingertips. Adam does NOT rub them; he warms them gently under his arms. Outro: "Don't rub."
4. **Power cut safety:** the kids shelter in the smallest room with the door shut and drafts blocked, NOT in a room with a gas appliance. Outro: "Never heat with an oven or grill indoors" (carbon monoxide).
5. **Never out alone at night:** Adam goes out only after a real grown-up (Dad, proven by his orange light) has called an ambulance and told him to, WITH his sister, holding on. Rule eight: "Together, with a grown-up."
6. **Kindness:** the old man who quietly shoveled everyone's walk for years was the one nobody checked on. The landing line is his, not the narrator's: "First time anyone knocked on mine." The CTA asks viewers to knock, with a grown-up, on the door of someone who lives alone.

## Villain design spec: MISTER SHIVER

He has no other name. The truck sign reads **MISTER SHIVER'S - Sweet Treats All Year!** The irony is that he is named Shiver, and what he does is stop your shivering.

Origin, kept mysterious and never shown: he comes only on nights as cold as the night in 1958 (minus thirty or colder), and he is gone by sunrise. He took Benny Kessler that night. Kessler's one-line answer, if ever needed in a later film: "He isn't a man. He's what the cold looks like when it wants to be let in."

Name check before upload: "Shiver" is also a character name in an unrelated game franchise (different archetype; low risk). Search the title and tags once before publishing, and never pair the name with any wording from the reference game.

### FORM 1: the friendly vendor

- About 6 ft 4 in, narrow-shouldered, slightly hunched, with a long neck. He is almost always seen from the chest up, framed by the serving window.
- The head is a smooth, egg-shaped bald dome, a little too big for the neck. Read test: in black against the pink window you should see a bald egg head, a raised waving hand and the bunting of mittens.
- The arms are slightly too long. When he "leans down. Down." (5:50) he bends far past what a man can do, hinging out of the window toward the snow.

**Colours**
- Skin: chalky, cold white (#E9E4DC) with blue-grey shadows (#B9C3CF). No flush on the cheeks or nose. Tiny frost flecks on the brow (he has no eyebrows).
- Eyes: small, round and bright blue (#4FB3FF) with a pinpoint highlight. Set close; they never blink.
- Smile: too wide, reaching nearly to the ears, with off-white teeth (#F3EEDC): two big buck front teeth with a gap between them, and a gap-toothed row below. Pale lips (#D9C6C6).
- Uniform: a vendor jacket in VERTICAL pink (#E58FB4) and white (#F7F2F4) stripes, buttoned to the throat, with a round white collar. Original additions (keep them, they make him ours): a small pink bow tie with a silver SNOWFLAKE pin (#C9D6E3), and a snowflake-shaped name badge reading SHIVER.
- Gloves: white KNITTED WOOL gloves (#EDEDED), like a child's winter gloves. They are the only warm-looking thing he wears.

**Behaviour and tells**
- A slow, over-long wave with one hand, which keeps going during the transformation.
- **The tell: he never makes a breath cloud**, even at minus forty, while every human in every outdoor shot puffs white breath. Animators: give EVERY human a breath puff outdoors (and indoors after the heat dies at 3:50), and NEVER give him one. His copies (Mom at the door) also have none.
- **The mitten bunting:** a line of single children's mittens strung across the inside of his serving window like party flags, one for each person he has taken. The oldest, at the LEFT end, is a small RED one (Benny's, 1958). At 7:20 Lina's mitten is added at the RIGHT end, "saving her a place" (a separate cutaway shot of the serving window; the leg carries it back over the roof). At 11:20 hers drops off into the snow. The red one stays.

**Voice:** a bright, sugary, sing-song TV-host voice. When he is losing, it slows and drops to a low whisper. When he imitates someone (Dad on the radio, Mom at the door, a little boy at the window), a faint chime runs UNDER the copy in the mix. The narration names the bells only where Adam hears them (the jammed radio); under the fake Dad they are AUDIO ONLY, a fair-play cue for attentive viewers.

### FORM 2: the skull with spider legs

**Silhouette and proportions**
- The face becomes a long, narrow skull-like mask about twice the length of the normal head, bone-white and hard. The jaw hangs down to the chest.
- Eight long brown jointed legs come out of his back, each about 3 times his height when stretched, with three joints each, like a harvestman's. They lift his torso clear of the truck, so he looks like a man hanging in the middle of a cage of legs.
- Read test: in silhouette against the snow, a high arch of jointed legs with a small striped body slung under it and a long pale head hanging down.

**Colours**
- Mask: bone white (#EFE9DD) with fine frost-crack lines (#9FB4C8), like ice on a pond.
- Eyes: deep dark sockets, then glowing RED (#FF2A2A) with a soft bloom.
- Mouth: a huge dark mouth (#120A10). The teeth are long, thin and see-through, like ICICLES (#DDEFFF, with a pale blue rim light). This is our winter signature and is not in the reference images. No gore, no blood and no tongue.
- Legs: brown (#6B4A32) with darker joints (#3E2A1C), frost rime on every knee (#DCE8F2), and needle-sharp pale tips that leave small round holes in the snow.
- The torn striped uniform stays on his body: the stripes split down the back where the legs come out.

**How he moves**
- He scuttles low and fast across snow, with legs stabbing left and right.
- He runs ALONG fences and over roofs.
- Drops from above: in front of the front door at 7:15 (right way up, jaw hanging); through the attic hatch at 8:25 (the ONLY upside-down entrance in the film, keep it fresh). At the door at 8:05 the face slides UP the glass, right way up.
- Clicks: every leg plant is a "click". Three clicks are the warning sound before he appears.
- **What he does to people who are together: he tries to SEPARATE them.** Legs spear Adam's coat to the ground, pry at Lina in his arms, reach BETWEEN the kids from the attic hatch, feel under the bathroom door for Lina's sock, hook the blanket and pull at the climax. ### His rules (story logic, kept consistent in the narration)
1. He comes only on nights as cold as 1958, and he is gone by sunrise (7:51 a.m. tonight). Kessler says so in the setup.
2. He cannot enter a warm home. He can kill the heat only in a house his "free one" has been BROUGHT INTO: Lina brings the cone in through the kitchen window, it shatters, and the furnace, the lights and the phone signal die. That is why rule 2 matters and why the rest of the street keeps its heat. Kessler's furnace failed on its own the night before: real cold needs no monster.
3. Once a house is as cold as outside, it counts as outside and he can get in (the attic, 8:15). The smallest room with the door shut and the drafts blocked is inside again: he can probe under the door but cannot enter.
4. He can only TAKE someone who is cold AND alone, at his window. This is never printed on a card. Adam learns it in the chase ("He can't take her while I'm holding her. So he wants me to let go.") and says it once at the climax ("Cold. Alone. He needs both."). Everything he does is aimed at separating people: separate rooms, sleep, prying legs, the hooked blanket, a voice calling one person to the window.
5. His "free one" always goes to whoever is coldest. The cold does the rest: sleepiness, feeling warm, mittens off, walking to his window. These are the real signs of hypothermia, shown without being named until the outro.
6. He imitates voices, but he makes no breath cloud and bells leak under every copy. He jams the radio when he wants to cut off help; when he is busy at Kessler's, the bells leave the static.
7. Everyone he takes leaves one mitten on his string. Nothing else is kept, and the interior of the truck is never shown.
8. His real strategy: fear keeps a whole street behind closed curtains, which leaves the loneliest person with nobody coming. Every time Adam turns toward Kessler's house, he is given something closer to be scared of.
9. He is beaten by togetherness, warmth and witnesses. If the person at his window is held (not alone), shivering again (fighting the cold) and seen (porch lights), the last note can't play and his jaw can't close. He retreats; he does not die.

## The 7 rules (verbatim, as in the narration)

Rule number one.
When you hear his song, close every curtain. If he can't see you, he can't call you.

Rule number two.
If he leaves you a free one, don't touch it, don't bring it in, and never taste it. Nothing he gives is free.

Rule number three.
If the heat goes out, put on every layer you own, and get under one blanket together. Nobody sleeps alone tonight.

Rule number four.
If you stop shivering, tell someone right away. Shivering means you're still fighting.

Rule number five.
If someone you love knocks, look for their breath. In this cold, everyone's breath makes a cloud. His doesn't.

Rule number six.
