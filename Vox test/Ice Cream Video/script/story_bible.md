## Logline

It is the coldest night in Larkfield since 1958. Twelve-year-old Adam is home alone with his six-year-old sister Lina, and he follows the seven rules that grumpy old Mr. Kessler tapes to every door on Birch Lane. A smiling pink-and-white ice cream truck plays its song street by street, and then it parks outside. All night the ice cream man hunts them: he leaves free cones that get inside, kills their heat, steals their father's voice, lures Lina to his window, and finally splits open into a spider-legged skull. Then Adam realises the hunt was never the point. The monster was keeping every house inside, so that nobody would cross the street to the old man who wrote the rules.

## The message in one sentence

Cold, like every predator, takes whoever is alone, so the bravest rule is the one you keep together: check on the neighbour nobody checks on.

Lessons the story ACTS OUT (named only in the short outro):
1. **Lures:** "free treat", "just come to the window", a familiar voice that says the opposite of what your parent said. Nothing a stranger gives is free. Check, stay inside, tell a grown-up.
2. **Hypothermia signs and first aid:** stopping shivering, sleepiness, feeling warm, taking mittens off, walking off. Adam calls for help first, then dry socks, layers, a blanket around her middle, body heat, and he stays with her. Shivering coming back is a good sign.
3. **Never out alone at night:** Adam calls 911 and Dad first, and goes out only on a grown-up's word, WITH his sister, holding on.
4. **Kindness:** the old man who quietly shoveled everyone's walk for years was the one nobody checked on. The landing line is his, not the narrator's: "First time anyone knocked on mine."

## Villain design spec: MISTER SHIVER

He has no other name. The truck sign reads **MISTER SHIVER'S - Sweet Treats All Year!** The irony is that he is named Shiver, and what he does is stop your shivering.

Origin, kept mysterious and never shown: he comes only in a cold snap cold enough to kill (minus thirty or colder), and he is gone by sunrise. He took Benny Kessler in the winter of 1958. Kessler's one-line answer, if ever needed in a later film: "He isn't a man. He's what the cold looks like when it wants to be let in."

### FORM 1: the friendly vendor

**Silhouette and proportions**
- About 6 ft 4 in, narrow-shouldered, slightly hunched, with a long neck. He is almost always seen from the chest up, framed by the serving window.
- The head is a smooth, egg-shaped bald dome, a little too big for the neck. Read test: in black against the pink window you should see a bald egg head, a raised waving hand and the bunting of mittens.
- The arms are slightly too long. When he "leans all the way down" (6:10) he bends far past what a man can do, hinging out of the window toward the snow.

**Colours**
- Skin: chalky, cold white (#E9E4DC) with blue-grey shadows (#B9C3CF). No flush on the cheeks or nose. There are tiny frost flecks on the brow (he has no eyebrows).
- Eyes: small, round and bright blue (#4FB3FF) with a pinpoint highlight. They are set close and never blink.
- Smile: too wide, reaching nearly to the ears, with off-white teeth (#F3EEDC): two big buck front teeth with a gap between them, and a gap-toothed row below. Pale lips (#D9C6C6).
- Uniform: a vendor jacket in VERTICAL pink (#E58FB4) and white (#F7F2F4) stripes, buttoned to the throat, with a round white collar. Original additions (keep them, they make him ours): a small pink bow tie with a silver SNOWFLAKE pin (#C9D6E3), and a snowflake-shaped name badge reading SHIVER.
- Gloves: white KNITTED WOOL gloves (#EDEDED), like a child's winter gloves. They are the only warm-looking thing he wears.

**Behaviour and tells**
- A slow, over-long wave with one hand, which keeps going during the transformation.
- **The tell: he never makes a breath cloud**, even at minus forty, while every human in every outdoor shot puffs white breath. Animators: give EVERY human a breath puff outdoors (and indoors after 4:13), and NEVER give him one.
- The mitten bunting: a line of single children's mittens is strung across the inside of his serving window like party flags, one for each person he has taken. The oldest, at the left end, is a small RED one (Benny's, 1958). At 7:30 Lina's mitten is added at the right end, beside it; at 11:10 hers drops off. The red one stays.

**Voice:** a bright, sugary, sing-song TV-host voice. When he is losing, it slows and drops to a low whisper. When he imitates someone (Dad, Benny), a faint chime always runs under the copy and the copy never says "Over".

### FORM 2: the skull with spider legs

**Silhouette and proportions**
- The face becomes a long, narrow skull-like mask about twice the length of the normal head, bone-white and hard. The jaw hangs down to the chest.
- Eight long brown jointed legs come out of his back, each about 3 times his height when stretched, with three joints each, like a harvestman's. They lift his torso clear of the truck, so he looks like a man hanging in the middle of a cage of legs.
- Read test: in silhouette against the snow, a high arch of jointed legs with a small striped body slung under it and a long pale head hanging down.

**Colours**
- Mask: bone white (#EFE9DD) with fine frost-crack lines (#9FB4C8), like ice on a pond.
- Eyes: deep dark sockets, then glowing RED (#FF2A2A) with a soft bloom.
- Mouth: a huge dark mouth (#120A10). The teeth are long, thin and see-through, like ICICLES (#DDEFFF, with a pale blue rim light). This is our winter signature and is not in the reference images. No gore, no blood and no tongue.
- Legs: brown (#6B4A32) with darker joints (#3E2A1C), frost rime on every knee (#DCE8F2), and needle-sharp pale tips that leave small round holes in the snow.
- The torn striped uniform stays on his body: the stripes split down the back where the legs come out.

**How he moves**
- He scuttles low and fast across snow, with legs stabbing left and right.
- He runs ALONG fences and over roofs, with his body upside down under the legs when it needs to be.
- He drops upside down from above (in front of the front door at 7:20; through the attic hatch at 8:35).
- Clicks: every leg plant is a "click". Three clicks are the warning sound before he appears.
- He NEVER grabs anyone who is being held. Legs stab the snow around a held child but never touch her. (Rule: he can take only someone cold AND alone, at his window.)

The owner's two reference images are fan art of the ice cream man from Keplerians' game "Ice Scream". We kept the ARCHETYPE (a creepy, too-friendly ice cream man who turns into a monster), which is common in horror and not protectable. We did not copy the character, the brand or the story.

### His rules (story logic, kept consistent in the narration)
1. He comes only in a killing cold snap, and he is gone by sunrise.
2. He cannot enter a warm home, so he kills the heat. Once a house is as cold as outside, it counts as outside, and he can get in (the attic, 8:20). A warm room with the door shut and the cracks blocked is inside again.
3. He can frighten, herd and chase, but he can only TAKE someone who is cold AND alone, at his window. He cannot take anyone who is being held. (That is why every chase ends in a near-miss.)
4. His "free one" always goes to whoever is coldest. One taste stops your shivering; then you feel warm and sleepy, take off your mittens and walk to his window. These are the real signs of hypothermia, shown without being named until the outro.
5. He imitates voices, but he can't make a breath cloud, never says "Over", and a chime leaks under the copy.
6. Everyone he takes becomes a note in his song and one mitten on his string.
7. His real strategy: fear keeps a whole street behind closed curtains, which leaves the loneliest person with nobody coming.
8. He is beaten by togetherness, warmth and witnesses. If the person at his window is held, shivering again and seen, the last note can't play and his jaw can't close. He retreats; he does not die.

## The 7 rules (verbatim, as in the narration)

Rule number one.
When you hear his song, close every curtain and get away from the windows. If he can't see you, he can't call you.

Rule number two.
If he leaves you a free one, don't touch it, don't bring it in, and never taste it. Nothing he gives is free.

Rule number three.
If the heat goes out, put on every layer you own, and get under one blanket together. He can only take someone who is cold, and alone.

Rule number four.
If you stop shivering, tell someone right away. Shivering means you're still fighting.

Rule number five.
If someone you love knocks, look for their breath. In this cold, everyone's breath makes a cloud. His doesn't.

Rule number six.
If the house gets as cold as outside, it isn't inside anymore. Find the warmest room, shut the door, and block the cracks.

Rule number seven.
Don't go outside before sunrise. Not for anyone. Not for anything. Not even for me.

Coda card (Adam's handwriting under Kessler's seven, card style matches but handwriting is a kid's):
Rule number eight.
Never go out for him. Go out for each other. Together.

Rule-card note: Kessler's cards are in shaky hand-drawn CAPITALS on lined paper with tape at the corners, signed "W.K." in the bottom corner.

Every rule is TRUE and is good real winter advice. He beats or bends each one (the cone gets inside anyway; he cuts the heat; he uses "stay inside" and "separate rooms" as Dad; he turns the house into "outside"). His real trick is rule seven itself.
