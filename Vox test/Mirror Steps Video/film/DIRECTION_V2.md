timings, prop names and the v1 shot ids quoted here.

`analysis/REF_V2_B.md` (sibling_stitched, vet_dog); `analysis/CRITIQUE_V1.md` (27 errors, comprehension gaps, scares).

## 0. The ten decisions (read this if nothing else)

1. **The Queen is rebuilt as a person in the cast's drawing language**: a tall, regal, beautiful woman, a playing-card queen.
   She has one readable signature: a crown of spades, a fan collar, a tiny spade beauty mark, lipstick-red fingertips.
   She goes wrong one feature at a time, on camera:
   - S1: a calm face;
   - S2: a lipstick smile drawn past her lips like little steps;
   - S3: that drawing splits open into a grin of flat human teeth, then the eyes go red;
   - S4: the jaw drops into a void with Noah's face in it.
   The full face arrives in the story at 531.7 s (76 %), not 283.7 s (40 %); before that it is seen only as a 0.42 s flash-forward in frame 0. v1's shark-tooth mask is retired.
2. **The world has one simple, visible rule set.**
   - The lipstick steps are a drawing of a real staircase.
   - The mirror becomes a doorway at the top of that staircase.
   - At the bottom is Cabin 7 flipped left-right, frozen at 6:59 AM fourteen years ago.
   - Every scene keeps that geography (`geography.jpg`).
3. **Four worlds, four looks, never confused**:
   - OURS: blue-violet night with a warm beam;
   - the STAIRS: plum, with red-edged treads and gold light from below;
   - THEIRS: golden, saturated and frozen;
   - MEMORY: sepia paper.
   A chip names place and time on every first shot after a switch.
4. **Relight everything.**
   - Night is coloured, never black: frames under luma 60 drop from 42.8 % to 10 % or fewer.
   - Colourfulness p50 rises from 16 to 45 or more.
   - The darkest and reddest tenth is 560-630 s, with 13-18 % red pixels.
5. **Every noun and every rule mechanism gets a picture**: 4-6 inserts, documents or diagrams per minute (v1: 2.7). The
   logic is drawn:
   - the game diagram;
   - numbers counted on the steps;
   - a side view of the glass;
   - THEIR FLOOR | OUR FLOOR;
   - the eye line;
   - OUR TIME | THEIR TIME;
   - the loop clock;
   - bell sound rings, fake against real;
   - 7:00 AM when the loop breaks;
   - the eighth line on the phone.
6. **Wides go from 8.5 % to 30-35 % of shots, and faces from 50 % to 25-30 %.** Each main set is a perspective room shot from
   6-12 angles, and every rule card and every side switch is followed by a wide.
7. **Faster and more alive.**
   - 430-450 shots (36-40 cuts per minute; v1 317), median 1.5 s, one clause = one shot = one caption.
   - Camera pushes about 3 %/s (v1 1.6), subject motion share 0.33 (v1 0.19), and 2-3 white flash or glitch cuts per minute (v1 0).
   - Long shots are spent only on the Queen's transformations (10 listed holds).
8. **The rule card becomes the mirror itself.**
   - The towel whips off it, "RULE" is written in lipstick, and the number stamps in.
   - Her reflection behind the glass comes closer on every card; the glass cracks and turns red by card 7.
9. **All 27 v1 errors are fixed** (section 6), plus 6 more found for this brief.
10. **Sound stays.** It is re-timed to the new cuts, with small hits for the new picture devices, and the quiet stretches
    are allowed to breathe (LRA about 5 LU at -14 LUFS).

**How a viewer tells THEIRS from MEMORY at a glance:**
- THEIRS is crisp, saturated, too clean and frozen.
- MEMORY is soft, sepia and grainy, and it moves.
- The same place appears in both (the cabin at morning). Never grade a memory shot `dawn_*`, and never grade a THEIRS shot `memory`.

**The side-switch cue.** On the first frame of any cut that crosses between OURS and THEIRS, a 3-frame glass glint plays:
a diagonal white streak at 60 % alpha and a 6 px chromatic fringe. That makes 18-22 uses in the film, and it is never used
inside a world.

## 3. The Queen of Spades: redesign spec

**Why this design.** Across the five references, and in our own approved police film, the scare is a plausible person in the
cast's own drawing style who becomes wrong one feature at a time:
- red irises with two highlights;
- veined whites;
- a grin of flat human teeth 0.5-0.75 of the face width;
- folds and tears;
- a jaw that hangs into a void while a loved one's voice comes out.

The stages are timed to the narration and held in the film's only long shots
(`td_villain_build.jpg`, `st_faces_build.jpg`, `sib_dad_turns_486-510s.jpg`, `sib_possessed.jpg`, `vet_faces.jpg`).

v1's Queen failed on four counts:
- a no-outline scratch ghost nobody could see;
- a generic shark-tooth mask shown six times with a frozen mouth;
- no Queen of Spades identity;
- the full face spent at 40 % of the film.

Two things are kept: lipstick red as her only colour, and the black high-collared dress.

**Our own motif.** She is the one who draws the steps. The lipstick that drew the staircase on the glass also draws her smile
wider, step by step, and that drawing is what opens.

### 3.1 Silhouette and body (readable at thumbnail size)
- **Height** 1.12x an adult cast member. Head 1/7.5 of her height (adults in the cast: about 1/6.5), so she reads taller and
  more elegant. Neck 1.25x, shoulders 0.9x Tess's, a cinched waist, arms to mid-thigh (1.15x), fingers 1.35x.
- **Four shapes make the silhouette**, like a court card:
  1. **A crown of spades**: 5 small black spade points on a thin silver band (`#c9ccd6`). The centre point is 0.22 of the head

## 8. For the owner: what changes and why (plain words)

- **The Queen of Spades is redrawn as a real, beautiful, regal woman**, a playing-card queen with a crown of spades, a tall
  black collar and red lipstick, drawn in the same style as the other characters.
  - She becomes frightening step by step, on screen. First she simply turns her head and never blinks. Then her lipstick smile
    is drawn too wide, like little steps up her cheeks. Then that drawing opens into a mouth full of teeth, and her eyes turn red.
  - In the end her jaw drops and your brother's voice comes out.
  - Her scariest face is saved for the last quarter of the film.
- **The story is easy to follow.**
  - The mirror, the doorway and the staircase are one place you can see.
  - The other side is your cabin flipped and frozen at 6:59 in the morning, fourteen years ago.
  - Every rule's logic is shown with a simple picture: counting the steps, the floor with and without footprints, the looping
    clock, the perfect fake bell against the cracked real one, and the clock finally reaching 7:00.
- **It looks much better.**
  - Night is deep blue-violet with warm flashlight light instead of murky black.
  - The other side is golden.
  - Memories look like old photos.
  - The ending climb is drenched in red.
  - The sets are full, real-looking rooms seen from many angles.
- **It is tighter and tenser.**
  - About 40% more shots, with constant motion inside each shot.
  - The long moments are kept only for her transformations.
  - Every scare is restaged so you actually see it: her sitting down, the real bell being struck, her foot on the step below
    yours, Tess yanking you out.
- **All the mistakes in version 1 are fixed** (33 of them, from the missing candy to the eighth step that never appeared on the
  phone).
- **The narration, voice and music stay.** The sound is only re-timed to the new picture, with a few new small hits.
