Wednesday, at my shoulder... she didn't blink.

We'd met on Monday.

Then Danny crossed the hall, first time in weeks, with the card.

Rule number one.

You don't have to answer after goodnight. Phone off. Sleep.

Wednesday night.

Every ping, one note of my song. Twelve messages. Thirty. Forty-seven.

I kept rule one. Phone off.

The dark screen lit up anyway.

Under the streetlight, Evie smiled up. At her feet, in big curly chalk...

Only me.

She didn't blink.

I switched off my light. Below me, our porch light clicked on. Evie lay down flat, like a chalk drawing... and slid away.

Thursday morning, our path was wet and clean.

Must have rained.

Bucket by the door. Mom on the phone, knuckles red.

Somebody had noticed Evie. Good.

On our wet step: one white sneaker print. Pointing in.

Forty-seven messages, in one night. Before rule two, one word in the comments.

If someone did that for you... would you feel Special, or Trapped?

Rule number two.

A gift is not a bill. If a present makes you feel you owe someone, give it back.

Inside my locked locker: a woven bracelet. Green and gold. Chalk dust on it.

Suddenly Evie was one inch from my shoulder. Not touching. Never touching.

She didn't. I did. It was Danny's bracelet.

So Danny gave it away. And I'd only ever messaged him a normal amount.

I kept it. Heavy as a stone.

Now I owed her.

That night, only me was halfway up our path. Evie on it, smiling up.

Her smile was a white chalk line now. Just past the corners.
