Rule number seven.

Don't keep it secret. Tell a grown-up. Save the messages. If they don't listen, tell another. Keep telling.

I stepped over the chalk. I didn't go to the door.

The kitchen light was already on. Mom, still dressed. Bucket by the door. Red knuckles.

I told her everything. The square. The bracelet. My password. Evie.

Behind me, the letterbox rattled. Chalk fingers wrote M... E... toward the kitchen.

The letters crept across the tiles... and stopped at her chair.

She'd said that on the phone every morning. To Danny's mom.

It never rained. Every dawn, Mom had scrubbed my handwriting off our path. She'd called Mrs. Rahman. She'd sat up every night, listening. And she'd kept asking, until I let her in.

Mom saved every message from Evie.

Each one a note of my song.

Then I scrolled to Danny. My thread. And stopped.

The same words. Word for word. Sent by me.

Forty-seven. In one night. A normal amount.

Under them, Danny's last message.

That blue front door was Danny's.

Sunday. Midnight. Chalk-white sleeves. Smiling up at his dark window, two words at my feet.

It was me.

Danny didn't go down. He woke his mom.

Mom didn't look away.

She opened our front door and stood beside me.

Evie was on our doormat, smiling ear to ear.

Mom looked right at her. Evie came no closer.

I took a breath.

Her song came, stretched thin.

Her colour drained to chalk-white, and the wind carried her off like dust.

At dawn, we scrubbed the path together. The hall floor, I did myself.

Lina sang new words to my old tune.

Monday, I told Mrs. Rahman too.

With Mom beside me, I sent Danny one message.

No reply. I didn't send another. Maybe he'd never answer. That had to be okay.

The hardest rule.
