Rule number three.

Your password is yours. Your location is for your parents only.

At lunch, Evie slid her phone across the table.

I typed mine in.

On my map, a white dot joined my blue one.

Her dot got everywhere first. The bus stop. The shop. Danny's street.

Two a.m. Her dot was on our house.

Then inside it.

Then in my room.

I swung my torch. Nobody.

Just one white chalk fingerprint... on the inside of my window.

Outside, only me had reached the foot of our step.

Friday. Mrs. Rahman, the counsellor, at her open door.

I walked past.

On my phone, mute icons. By Mom. By my sister, Lina. By Danny.

I hadn't muted them.

Rule number four.

If someone keeps turning up where you are, tell a grown-up. Never go alone to sort it out.

Mom's car. Red light. Evie, on the pavement, right by my window.

Green light. Gone.

Next red light. There.

She never ran.

At break, on the swings, she sang my song. Every word perfect.

I found Danny.

He just blinked.

At sunset, I went to sort it out. Alone. Her white prints led to the square.

It was covered in chalk. My handwriting. My curly M's.

A name, rubbed out: Danny. Written over it: Adam. And under that...

Only me.

From the square, slowed down, came my song. In my seven-year-old voice.

I told you nobody was cruel enough to try it.

I was.

Last Sunday. I'd chalked Danny's name. I thought it hadn't worked.

The chalk slid out, flat across the playground... and stood up.

Evie. Washed in red.

Arms spread across the gate. Never touching.

Floodlight. The caretaker, squinting right at us. She was flat chalk again.

I ran.

That night, only me was on our step. Under it, one empty chalk line. Waiting for a name.
