If someone you met three days ago says you're their favourite person in the whole world... don't say it back. Especially if she doesn't blink.

If your phone is off... and it still lights up: "I can see your light's still on"... don't look out the window.

And if you look down at midnight... and someone at your blue front door is smiling up, two words chalked at their feet...

...don't go down. Wake a grown-up.

I know who it is.

When I was twelve, my best friend handed me seven rules on a card. His mom's purple handwriting. At the top, one name, scribbled out in blue pen.

That week, a new girl decided I'd be her best friend. Only hers. By Saturday midnight, she was on our doormat, my sister was reading a name under the landing light... and I was halfway down the stairs to let her in.

Danny had been my best friend since we were seven. Now he ate lunch with Kofi and Sam. Chewed blue pen behind his ear.

I ate alone.

At seven, on the swings, I made up a song for him.

I made it up. Nobody else knows it.

Danny's wrist was bare. The green-and-gold bracelet I wove him in third grade... gone.

Monday, Mom held up my grey hoodie. Sleeves white to the elbow.

She looked a beat too long.

Behind our school: an old hopscotch. All new paint, except the top square. Faded red.

The Keeping Square.

Hop it alone at sunset. Chalk a name, and under it, only me. That person will never leave you.

Nobody was cruel enough to try it.

In the square, someone had chalked a little girl. Two braids. A smile that didn't stop.

That day, a new girl sat behind me. Freckles. Gap-tooth grin. Braids tied with faded red ribbons.

By Tuesday, she was waiting at my locker. She knew my favourite crisps.

Somebody waiting for me, for once.
