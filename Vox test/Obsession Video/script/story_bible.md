Narration: `narration_en.txt`, 2,097 words (about 12.5 minutes). The first beat plan is in `outline.md`; where they differ, this bible and the narration win.

**Revision 2 (after two critiques):**
- The hook no longer says whose rules they were. It ends on what it cost ("halfway down the stairs to let her in"). "I never asked whose." is cut; the blue scribble still shows on screen.
- The cold open's second line is now a scare (the switched-off phone lights up). The third line puts the viewer at Danny's window, looking down.
- The setup spreads its clues out. The hoodie scene comes before the hopscotch legend, "a normal amount" moved into the rule 2 misdirect, the pickup wave is cut, and a chalk girl is drawn in the Keeping Square.

- There is now one rule for when Evie goes flat: **a grown-up's look holds her back.** Mom's porch light (rule 1), the caretaker squinting (rule 4), Mom's chair and Mom's look at the door (rule 7). Telling everything is what drains her.
- The climax keeps Evie on screen: the letterbox writes M... E... toward the kitchen, the letters stop at Mom's chair, and the pings keep coming while Mom saves.
- No romance can be read in: Adam types "You're my only friend.", and Evie asks him to write his name "Under ONLY ME" (the ritual words, never a heart, never a plus sign, never two names as a pair).

- Red is no longer a whole-frame wash. Door shots are framed from an upstairs window looking down, with ONLY ME as the main shape (see Look rules).

## Title
**If a Girl Is OBSESSED With Being Your Only Friend... DON'T WRITE YOUR NAME**

Alternates:
- If a New Girl Says You're Her Favourite Person in the World... DON'T SAY IT BACK
- If "ONLY ME" Is Chalked Closer to Your Door Every Night... DON'T ANSWER
- If Chalk Fingers Come Through Your Letterbox Writing ONLY... DON'T GO DOWN

**Thumbnail** (command: DON'T WRITE YOUR NAME): a warm, lamp-lit hall floor seen from the stairs. Long chalk-white fingers reach through the letterbox and write O-N-L-Y across the boards. In the slot itself, only Evie's chalk smile shows, ear to ear. A chalk stub lies on the bottom stair. There is no figure at a door, no red wash and no marks on the ground except the legible letters.

**Logline:** A lonely 12-year-old's best friend has pulled away. The week a new girl decides he is her favourite person in the whole world, she floods his phone, takes his password, turns up at every red light and chalks ONLY ME one step nearer his door each night. Then he finds out who made her, and whose words she is using.

**Week:**
- **Sunday, before the film** (seen only in reveals): at sunset Adam chalks DANNY / ONLY ME in the Keeping Square. At midnight he stands at Danny's blue door, smiling up, ONLY ME chalked at his feet, and sends 47 messages. Danny answers "Please stop. You're scaring me.", doesn't go down, and wakes his mom. She calls Adam's mom.
- **Monday:** the hoodie at breakfast. Evie arrives.
- **Tuesday:** the locker and the crisps.
- **Wednesday:** "favourite person". Danny brings the card, with Mrs. Rahman nodding from her doorway behind him. That night, rule 1.
- **Thursday:** the wet path at dawn, Mom's phone call and the sneaker print. The locker bracelet (rule 2). At lunch, the password (rule 3). That night ONLY ME is halfway up the path; at 2 a.m. it reaches the foot of the step.
- **Friday:** Mrs. Rahman's door and the mute icons. Then rule 4: the car, the swings, the square at sunset. That night ONLY ME is on the step, with an empty line under it.
- **Saturday:** dinner (rule 5) and the doorstep. At midnight, the letterbox (rule 6), then the kitchen and the door (rule 7).
- **After:** Sunday dawn, the scrub. Monday, Mrs. Rahman. Weeks later, the lunch table. The coda is "last week", about 25 years on.

Day labels go on screen in a small chalk font.

## The girl: Evie ("Like forever")

**Origin:** The Keeping Square is the faded red top square of an old hopscotch by the school's back fence, the one square the new paint never covered. The legend: hop it alone at sunset, chalk a name, write ONLY ME under it, and that person will never leave you. Evie is made of every ONLY ME ever chalked there. In the setup she is still a drawing in the square: a little chalk girl with two braids and a smile that doesn't stop. She never changes the person you name. Instead she sends back someone who holds on to YOU the way you held on (the team's backstory; the film never says it aloud). Adam chalked DANNY; the square rubbed it out and wrote ADAM.

**Her rules (four limits, all fair play):**
1. **She cannot touch anyone.** A touch would smudge her. She blocks with her arms wide, reaches, and stands one inch too close. It is said twice: "Not touching. Never touching." and "Never touching."
2. **She comes only as close as his yeses let her.** Each warning sign he goes along with moves her chalk ONLY ME one stretch nearer (table below).
3. **She knows only what Adam knows.** Every word she uses is one he once used: his crisps, his song, his messages to Danny.
4. **A grown-up's look holds her back; telling drains her.** She lives where grown-ups aren't looking. By day she passes for an ordinary new girl. Her chalk night-side goes flat the moment a grown-up looks her way: Mom's porch light in rule 1, the caretaker squinting under his floodlight in rule 4. By Saturday she is strong enough that Mom's look only stops her (the letters stop at Mom's chair; on the doormat she "came no closer"). What ends her is Adam telling everything, with that grown-up beside him, and saying no. Children's looks don't count: Lina sees her and can't stop her. In every other shot, the grown-ups in frame are facing away (Mom's back to the dinner window, Mom's eyes on the road at the red lights).

**Goal:** For him to write his own name under ONLY ME, with her chalk, of his own free will. Then he becomes what she is.

**Beaten by real-world moves:** He steps over the chalk and goes to Mom, not the door. He tells her everything. She saves, blocks and reports. He says a kind, clear no with Mom beside him, and then he tells the counsellor too. The caretaker paints the square over: an ordinary grown-up fix, not a counter-charm.

### Design notes for animators (our own design)
- **Look:** Shorter than Adam, freckles, gap-tooth grin. Two dark braids tied with faded red ribbons, the exact red of the Keeping Square (an origin seed). A long-sleeved white blouse with a high round collar, an ankle-length navy pinafore dress and navy leggings. White sneakers that leave white chalk prints. Chalk dust on her fingertips and a worn chalk stub always in one hand.
- **Modest and kid-safe:** Arms, legs and neckline always covered, in every form. She never touches anyone, and nothing she does is romantic: no hearts, no blowing kisses, no lovey poses. Her fixation is "best friend, only me".
- **No harm on screen:** no blood, no cuts, no claws, no teeth-baring, and no cracking or broken-bone sounds when her neck stretches. The stretch is a smooth, rubbery chalk-drawing stretch.
- **Never copy the reference stills:** no long loose dark hair, no strappy top or bare shoulders, no open-mouth scream-grin, no necklace, no red smears on the face. No porch with columns, no white door, no claw scratches on the ground: our marks are always the legible words ONLY ME.

### Look rules (so we never rebuild the reference poster)
- **Red is the Keeping Square's faded red and nothing else:** the square, her ribbons, and the sunset glow at the school when she stands up ("Washed in red."). Night scenes at the house are blue-black with warm window light. There is never a whole-frame red wash.
- **Door shots are framed from an upstairs window, looking down.** ONLY ME is the biggest shape in frame and the figure is small. Never shoot a lone figure square-on at a front door at eye level. This applies to the cold open, the rule 5 doorstep, the green door and the replay at Danny's blue door.
- The doormat moment in rule 7 is shot from inside the hall, past Mom and Adam's backs, with the door frame cropping Evie.

## The boy, family and helpers
- **Adam (12; the narrator as a boy):** Likeable and lonely, messy dark hair. His grey hoodie has sleeves white to the elbow in the Monday scene. At seven he made up the song for Danny. He isn't bad: he held on too tight and can't see it in himself. He is helped, never shamed.
- **Mom (40s):** Warm, tired and sharp, hair tied back, a long-sleeved work shirt. She has known since Danny's mom's first call, and she is working the whole time:
  - Every dawn she scrubs ONLY ME, in her son's handwriting, off the path (red knuckles, bucket by the door).
  - She calls Mrs. Rahman.
  - She sits up every night listening. R1: it is her hand on the porch-light switch (shown only as the light clicking on). R3, picture only: at 2 a.m. a strip of hall light shows under Adam's door, with Mom's shadow on the stairs.
  - She keeps asking: "Anything you want to tell me?" on Monday and again on Saturday, with her fork down.
  - In the kitchen, picture only: she taps Save, then Block and Report on Evie's thread.
  - After the reveal she doesn't look away, and she names both truths: Danny was right, and Adam is not bad. At the door she stands beside him and looks right at Evie.
- **Lina (7):** His little sister, in pigtails and oversized long pyjamas, with crayons. She keeps bringing him drawings and he keeps shutting her out. She reads the name on the card and sings the healed words.
- **Danny (12):** A bit shorter than Adam, glasses, a chewed blue pen behind his ear, a bare wrist. He is kind. He set a boundary, told his mom, kept every real gift, and crossed out Adam's name so the card could still help him.
- **Danny's mom:** Never speaks. She wrote the rules in purple ink. In the cold-open replay she is the silhouette at the upstairs window, phone to her ear.
- **Mrs. Rahman:** The school counsellor, calm and unshockable. Navy hijab, long grey jacket, lanyard. Her office has a plant and a tissue box. Picture only, on Wednesday: she stands in her open doorway behind Danny as he crosses the hall with the card, and nods. A grown-up set this up; Danny is not handling it alone.
- **Background, silent:** Kofi and Sam (Danny's new friends); the caretaker (squinting under his floodlight in R4, and seen from behind rolling fresh paint over the square in the ending); grown Adam (only his hands, a phone and a bucket, in the coda).

Nobody is mocked, shamed or punished. Everyone is modestly dressed, and Adam and Evie never touch at any point.

## The motif: "Stick Like Glue"
Adam's own rhyme, made up at seven for Danny. It is our original four-note playground chant (ours to compose), with one soft chalk tap on every beat. There is no oath in it.

> Me and you, and you and me,
> stick like glue, and you will see.
> Everywhere you go, I'm near.
> Only me. I'm here, I'm here.

Healed version (same tune, Lina):

> Me and you, and others too,
> friends for me and friends for you.
> Go wherever, near or far.
> I'll still care, wherever you are.

## Safety and originality check
- **No romance:** Evie's fixation is "best friend, only me", and the friend Adam held on to is a boy. Adam's line to her is "You're my only friend." Her ritual is a name under the words ONLY ME, never two names as a pair, with no hearts and no plus signs. The words crush, girlfriend and love never appear.
- **No touching:** Nobody touches anybody. The chalk is dropped through the slot, never handed over.
- **Modest and gentle:** Everyone is dressed modestly. Chalk replaces blood and claws. There are no injuries, weapons or deaths; she drains to dust and blows away. The song carries no oath.
- **The wish is the wrong thing:** It hurt Danny and Adam's family ("Nobody was cruel enough to try it."). The fix is real-world: tell Mom, save, block and report, the counsellor, a kind clear no, and giving Danny space with no promise he'll come back ("That had to be okay.").
- **It is never the victim's fault:** Mom says it outright: "Danny was right to say no." Danny told his mom, and a grown-up (Mrs. Rahman) set up the card, so no child is left to handle it alone.
- **Compassion:** Adam is helped, not shamed ("And you're not bad."), and even Evie gets a kind goodbye ("I hope you find lots of people."). The parents and helpers are loving, competent and active.
- **Reference film:** No shop-bought wish toy, no music shop, no workplace romance, none of its names, no blood. We keep only the feeling: someone who adores you far too much, a smile that grows too wide, always there, always waiting outside. We drop the poster's layout: no lone figure square-on at a front door, no whole-frame red wash and no claw marks. Door shots look down from an upstairs window, and the thumbnail is the letterbox.
- **Our earlier films:** The rules come from a friend's mom, never from the monster. Nothing fades, there are no reflections, the chalk is words and never steps, the ending threatens no child of the narrator, and there is no "just you, our secret" line.
