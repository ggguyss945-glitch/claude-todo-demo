## Title
**If a Girl Is OBSESSED With Being Your Only Friend... DON'T WRITE YOUR NAME**

Alternates:
- If a New Girl Says You're Her Favourite Person in the World... DON'T SAY IT BACK
- If "ONLY ME" Is Chalked Closer to Your Door Every Night... DON'T ANSWER
- If a Girl Who Smiles Too Wide Is on Your Doormat at Midnight... DON'T WRITE YOUR NAME

Thumbnail command: DON'T WRITE YOUR NAME. Evie stands on a red-washed doormat at our GREEN door. Her chalk smile runs ear to ear, ONLY ME is chalked at her feet, and a chalk stub lies on the mat.

**Logline:** A lonely 12-year-old's best friend has pulled away. The week a new girl decides he is her favourite person in the whole world, she floods his phone, takes his password, turns up at every red light and chalks ONLY ME one step nearer his door each night. Then he finds out who made her, and whose words she is using.

**Week:**
- Sunday, before the film (seen only in reveals): at sunset Adam chalks DANNY / ONLY ME in the Keeping Square. At midnight he stands at Danny's blue door and sends 47 messages. Danny answers "Please stop. You're scaring me." and wakes his mom, who calls Adam's mom.
- Monday: the hoodie at breakfast; Evie arrives.
- Tuesday: the locker and the crisps.
- Wednesday: "favourite person"; Danny's card. That night, rule 1.
- Thursday: the wet path at dawn and Mom's phone call. The locker bracelet (rule 2). Lunch, the password (rule 3). That night ONLY ME is halfway up the path; at 2 a.m. it reaches the foot of the step.
- Friday: Mrs. Rahman's door and the mute icons. Then rule 4: the car, the swings, the square at sunset. That night ONLY ME is on the step.
- Saturday: dinner (rule 5), the doorstep. Midnight, the letterbox (rule 6), then the kitchen and the door (rule 7).
- After: Sunday dawn, the scrub. Monday, Mrs. Rahman. Weeks later, the lunch table. The coda is "last week", about 25 years on.

Day labels go on screen in a small chalk font.

## The girl: Evie ("Like forever")

**Origin:** The Keeping Square is the faded red top square of an old hopscotch by the school's back fence, the one square the new paint never covered. The legend: hop it alone at sunset, chalk a name, write ONLY ME under it, and that person will never leave you. Evie is made of every ONLY ME ever chalked there. She never changes the person you name. She sends back someone who holds on to YOU as tightly as you held on. Adam chalked DANNY; the square rubbed it out and wrote ADAM.

**Her rules (four limits, all fair play):**
1. **She cannot touch anyone.** A touch would smudge her. She blocks with her arms wide, reaches and stands one inch too close. Said twice: "Not touching. Never touching." and "Never touching."
2. **She comes only as close as his yeses let her.** Each warning sign he goes along with moves her chalk ONLY ME one stretch nearer (table below).
3. **She knows only what Adam knows.** Every word she uses is one he once used: his crisps, his song, his messages to Danny.
4. **Secrets feed her; telling drains her.** Once a grown-up has heard everything and stands with him, the red wash stops at that grown-up's shoulder.

**Goal:** For him to write his own name under ONLY ME, with her chalk, of his own free will. Then he becomes what she is.
**Beaten by real-world moves:** He steps over the chalk and goes to Mom, not the door. He tells her everything, she saves the messages, and he says a kind, clear no with Mom at his shoulder. Then he tells the counsellor too. The caretaker paints the square over: an ordinary grown-up fix, not a counter-charm.

Chalk dust on her fingertips and a worn chalk stub always in one hand.
- **Modest and kid-safe:** Arms, legs and neckline always covered, in every form. She never touches anyone, and nothing she does is romantic: no hearts, no blowing kisses, no lovey poses. Her fixation is "best friend, only me".
- **No harm on screen:** no blood, no cuts, no claws, no teeth-baring, and no cracking or broken-bone sounds when her neck stretches. The stretch is a smooth, rubbery chalk-drawing stretch.
- **Never copy the reference stills:** no long loose dark hair, no strappy top or bare shoulders, no open-mouth scream-grin, no necklace, no red smears on the face. No porch with columns or white door, and no claw scratches on the ground: our marks are always the legible words ONLY ME.

## The boy, family and helpers
- **Adam (12; narrator as a boy):** Likeable and lonely, messy dark hair. His grey hoodie has sleeves white to the elbow in the Monday scene. At seven he made up the song for Danny. He isn't bad; he held on too tight and can't see it in himself. He is helped, never shamed.
- **Mom (40s):** Warm, tired and sharp, hair tied back, long-sleeved work shirt.
  - She has known since Danny's mom's first call.
  - Every dawn she scrubs ONLY ME, in her son's handwriting, off the path: red knuckles, bucket by the door.
  - She calls Mrs. Rahman, then waits for Adam to let her in. That is the opposite of Evie, who forces her way in.
  - At the door she stands at his shoulder.
- **Lina (7):** His little sister, in pigtails and oversized long pyjamas, with crayons. She keeps bringing him drawings and he keeps shutting her out. She reads the name on the card and sings the healed words.
- **Danny (12):** A bit shorter than Adam, glasses, a chewed blue pen behind his ear, a bare wrist. He is kind. He set a boundary, told his mom, kept every real gift and still helped Adam.
- **Danny's mom:** Never speaks. She used to wave at pickup and now looks away. She is the silhouette at the upstairs window, with a phone, in the cold-open replay. She wrote the rules in purple ink.
- **Mrs. Rahman:** The school counsellor, calm and unshockable. Navy hijab, long grey jacket, lanyard. Her office has a plant and a tissue box.
- **Background, silent:** Kofi and Sam (Danny's new friends), the caretaker (seen from behind, rolling fresh paint over the square), and grown Adam (only his hands, a phone and a bucket, in the coda).

Nobody is mocked, shamed or punished. Everyone is modestly dressed, and there is no touching between Adam and Evie at any point.

## Outro checklist (on screen, matching the spoken words)
1. **Too much, too fast:** nonstop messages, gifts that make you feel you owe them.
2. **Wanting you all to themselves:** turning up uninvited, putting down your family and friends.
3. **Your passwords are yours.** Your location is for your parents.
4. **"It's your fault" is never true.** Say no, don't answer, block and report. Saying no isn't cruel.
5. **Don't keep it secret.** Tell a grown-up. Save the messages. Keep telling.
6. **Can't stop checking on someone?** That's holding on, not caring. Tell a grown-up. Asking for help is brave.

## Safety and originality check
- **No romance:** Evie's fixation is "best friend, only me", and the friend Adam held on to is a boy. The words crush, girlfriend and love never appear.
- **No touching:** Nobody touches anybody. The chalk is dropped through the slot, never handed over.
- **Modest and gentle:** Everyone is dressed modestly. Chalk replaces blood and claws. There are no injuries, weapons or deaths; she drains to dust and blows away.
- **The wish is the wrong thing:** It hurt Danny and Adam's family. The fix is real-world: tell Mom, save the messages, the counsellor, a kind clear no, and giving Danny space.
- **Compassion:** Adam is helped, not shamed, and even Evie gets a kind goodbye ("I hope you find lots of people."). The parents and helpers are loving and competent.
- **Reference film:** No shop-bought wish toy, no music shop, no workplace romance, none of its names, no blood. We keep only the feeling: the too-wide smile, a still figure at a door at night, red nights and marks on the ground.
- **Our earlier films:** The rules come from a friend's mom, never from the monster. Nothing fades, there are no reflections, the chalk is words and never steps, and the ending threatens no child of the narrator.
