DESCRIPTION
If a girl is obsessed with being your only friend, don't write your name.

When I was twelve, my best friend handed me seven rules on a card. His mom's purple handwriting. At the top, one name, scribbled out in blue pen.

That week, a new girl decided I was her favourite person in the whole world. Evie. "Like forever." She never blinked. She sent forty-seven messages in one night. She knew my secret song. And every night, two words in chalk came one step closer to my front door: ONLY ME.

Then I found out who made her. And whose words she was using.

The warning signs in one line: too much too fast, wanting you all to themselves, asking for your passwords or your location, turning up uninvited, putting down your family, and "it's your fault" are signs of control, not care. You can say no, not answer, block and report. Tell a grown-up and keep telling. And if you can't stop checking on someone, that's holding on, not caring: asking for help is brave.
