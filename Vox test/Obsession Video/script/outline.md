# Obsession Video: beat outline

## Title
**If a Girl Is OBSESSED With Being Your Only Friend... DON'T WRITE YOUR NAME**

Alternates:
- If a New Girl Says You're Her Favourite Person in the World... DON'T SAY IT BACK
- If "ONLY ME" Is Chalked Closer to Your Door Every Night... DON'T ANSWER
- If a Girl Who Smiles Too Wide Is on Your Doormat at Midnight... DON'T WRITE YOUR NAME

Thumbnail command: DON'T WRITE YOUR NAME. Evie stands on a red-washed doormat with her chalk smile reaching her ears and ONLY ME chalked at her feet. A stub of chalk lies on the mat.

## Judging

| Concept | Creep/tension | Twists | Lesson woven in | Halal/kid-safe/respect | Originality | Showability | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: Evie / chalk Keeping Square | 9 | 9 | 10 | 9 | 9 | 8 | **54** |
| B: Mira / paper chatterbox | 9 | 9 | 8 | 8 | 6 | 8 | 48 |
| C: Ivy / paper Keeper | 8 | 8 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 48 |

**Winner: A.** It is the only concept where every rule works twice. Each rule protects Adam from Evie, and each one was first written to protect Danny from Adam. So the vetted lesson has both halves: how to spot obsession, and what to do if you are the one holding on. Both halves live inside every beat, not just in the outro.
- The chalk ONLY ME creeps one stretch nearer with each yes. It is a scare tracker any viewer can read in one frame, and it gives us the poster image (a still figure at a door at night, in red, with marks on the ground) without copying it.
- Its twists climb a staircase: she learned the song from me, then I made the wish, then the rules were about me, then those were my words and that was me at the door. After the dark turns come two warm ones: Mom already knew, and Danny protected me.

**Why not B:** A lonely boy makes a wish on an object that one particular girl will care about him "more than anything in the world", and she is taken over. That is the reference film's plot moved to a school, and it reuses the film's key phrase. A real 12-year-old missing for three nights, with a police search, is also heavy for a kids' film. The ending pairs boy and girl ("Can I sit here?") in a way that could read as romance.

**Why not C:** The rules in the obsessed girl's own handwriting are close to B. They also lean toward our Doctor device, where the rules come from the monster. Ivy goes passive in rules 5 to 7 while Eli takes over. "I'd never seen one before" is a flat narrator lie. B and C both use a paper fortune-teller toy, and fortune-telling is haram-adjacent.

**Fixes to A:**
- **No pinky swear.** It is a hand touch between the boy and the girl, even though it is never taken. Her goal becomes "Write your name. Under mine." She drops the chalk stub through the letterbox and never touches him. The cold open and the motif change to match.
- **New wardrobe.** A cardigan buttoned to the collar echoes the Blank from Buttons, so Evie gets a new outfit (below).
- **She doesn't crumble.** A girl-shaped figure crumbling could read as a child dying. Instead her colour drains to chalk-white and the wind carries the dust back toward the school. The caretaker then paints over the Keeping Square. That is an ordinary grown-up fix, not a counter-charm.
- **Cheaper turning-up montage.** Lina's play and the dentist's window would need two extra sets, so they are cut. The montage becomes red lights from Mom's car (graft from B) plus the swings.
- **New rule 5 line.** "Your mom reads your phone" contradicted Mom, who waits to be let in. It becomes "Your mom's always on the phone about you", which also seeds T4.
- **The narrator's lie is fair.** "Nobody was dumb enough to try it" is paid off with "I was." Mom never explains a sign away. Adam is the one who says "Must have rained."
- **Seven twists tightened.** T5 and T6 land together in rule 7. The old ending stinger is merged with the coda from C.

**Grafts:**
- **From B:**
  - At every red light, Evie stands on the pavement right beside his car window, and she never runs.
  - The trap is in the wish itself: "Someone who can never leave isn't a friend. They're a prisoner."
  - The red wash stops at the shoulder of a grown-up who is standing with him.
  - The last line: "...ask them what happens when you say no."
- **From C:**
  - Mom's breakfast phone calls ("I know. I'll talk to him. Thank you for telling me.") were with Danny's mom.
  - Danny's last message in the old thread: "Please stop. You're scaring me."
  - The counsellor is Mrs. Rahman, in a navy hijab.
  - The grown narrator's honest coda: the pull still comes, and he puts the phone down.

## The villain: Evie ("Like forever")

**Origin:** The Keeping Square is the faded red top square of an old hopscotch by the back fence of the school playground. It is the one square the new paint never covered. The legend says: hop it alone at sunset, chalk a name, write ONLY ME under it, and that person will never leave you.

She is made of every ONLY ME ever chalked there. She never changes the person you name. She sends back someone who holds on to YOU exactly as tightly as you held on to them. She is a mirror.

Adam chalked DANNY. The square rubbed it out and wrote ADAM.

**Her four limits:**
1. **She cannot touch anyone.** A touch would smudge her. So she blocks with her arms wide, reaches, and stands one inch too close.
2. **She comes only as close as his yeses let her.** Every warning sign he goes along with moves her chalk ONLY ME one stretch nearer: pavement, path, step, doorstep, doormat, hall.
3. **She knows only what Adam knows.** Every word she uses is one he once used.
4. **Secrets feed her; telling drains her.** Once a grown-up has been told everything and stands beside him, the red wash stops at that grown-up's shoulder.

**Goal:** For him to write his own name under ONLY ME, in her chalk, of his own free will. Then he becomes what she is: chalk, waiting on someone's path at midnight forever.

**Defeat:** The real-world moves. He refuses the chalk, tells Mom everything, and the messages are saved. He says a kind, clear no while a grown-up stands with him. With nothing left to hold on to, she drains to dust. The grown-ups paint the square over.

**Look (our own, modest):**
- Shorter than Adam, with freckles and a gap-tooth grin.
- Two dark braids tied with faded red ribbons, the exact red of the Keeping Square. This is a seed for her origin.
- A long-sleeved white blouse with a high round collar, under an ankle-length navy pinafore dress, with navy leggings.
- White sneakers that leave white chalk prints.
- Chalk dust on her fingertips, and a worn stub of chalk always in her hand.

**Her smile (the key image):** From rule 2 her smile is a smooth white chalk line drawn over her mouth, and every night it is redrawn wider. It never looks like a cut: no red, no teeth, no gash. It is a crayon smile on a drawing.
- R1: her real smile.
- R2: just past the corners of her mouth.
- R3: mid-cheek.
- R4: wider.
- R5: almost at her ears.
- R6 and R7: ear to ear.

**Forms:**
- **NORMAL** (Setup, Monday and Tuesday, daylight): she blinks, bounces, looks away and laughs. Her ribbons are neat.
- **OBSESSED** (from "favourite person" in Setup through R4 daytime):
  - Head tilted, never blinks, and holds her smile too long.
  - She is already in the background of each shot before he notices her, with phone glow under her chin.
  - Her braids slowly unravel, and white sneaker prints are left wherever she's been.
- **CREEPY** (nights; a hint in R2, full form from R4 sunset):
  - She goes flat like a chalk drawing to slide across the ground, then stands up (R1 exit, R4).
  - Her neck bends up toward his window (R5).
  - Her fingers stretch impossibly long through the letterbox (R6).
  - In R7 she is at full width on the doormat. Then her colour drains to white and she drifts off as dust.
  - Red wash on all her night scenes from R4 sunset onward.

**Chalk tracker:** It is never drawn as steps or footprints, so it does not repeat Mirror Steps. It is the two words ONLY ME, with curly M's, and she stands on them.

| Night | Where ONLY ME is | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wed (R1, kept) | On the pavement at the end of the path | The starting point. He did nothing wrong. |
| Thu (R2 and R3 broken) | Halfway up the path, then at the foot of the step | He kept the gift and gave his password |
| Fri (R4 broken) | On the step | He went alone |
| Sat dinner (R5 broken) | On the doorstep | "Only you." |
| Sat midnight (R6) | On the doormat, then O-N-L-Y on the hall floor inside | The guilt is working |

Every morning until Saturday the path is wet and clean. Adam says "Must have rained." It never rained (T5).

## The boy, family and helpers

**Adam (12):** Likeable and lonely. Messy dark hair and a grey hoodie whose sleeves turn chalk-white in the seed scene. At seven he made up a rhyme for his best friend Danny. He isn't bad; he held on too tight and can't see it in himself. He is helped, never shamed.

**Mom (40s):** Warm, tired and sharp. Hair tied back, long-sleeved work shirt.
- She has known since Danny's mom's first call.
- Every dawn she found ONLY ME on their own path in her son's handwriting, scrubbed it off (red knuckles, a bucket by the door) and said nothing hurtful.
- She called Mrs. Rahman, then waited for Adam to let her in. That is the opposite of Evie, who forces her way in.
- At the door she stands at his shoulder: "He said no."

**Lina (7):** His little sister, in pigtails and oversized pyjamas, with crayons. She keeps bringing him drawings and he keeps shutting the door. She is the one who reads the name on the card. At dawn she sings the healed words.

**Danny (12):** Shorter than Adam, glasses, a chewed blue pen behind his ear, a bare wrist. He is kind. He set a boundary ("Please stop. You're scaring me."), told his mom, kept every real gift, and still helped Adam. He has two new friends, Kofi and Sam (silent background figures).

**Danny's mom:** Never speaks. She used to wave at pickup and now looks away. She wrote the card in purple ink. In the cold-open replay she is the silhouette at the upstairs window, with a phone to her ear.

**Mrs. Rahman:** The school counsellor, calm and unshockable. Navy hijab, long grey jacket, lanyard. Her office has a plant and a tissue box.

Nobody is mocked, shamed or punished.

## The motif: "Stick Like Glue"

Adam's own rhyme, made up at seven for Danny. It is our own four-note playground chant (ours to compose), with one soft chalk tap on every beat:

> Me and you, and you and me,
> stick like glue, and you will see.
> Everywhere you go, I'm there.
> Only me. I swear, I swear.

**How it warps:**
1. **Setup flashback:** little Adam sings it sweetly to little Danny on the swings, in a warm palette.
2. **R1:** each of the 47 message pings is one note, so the tune loops over and over in the dark.
3. **R4, by day:** Evie sings it perfectly on the swings.
4. **R4, sunset:** it rises out of the Keeping Square in Adam's own seven-year-old voice, slowed down.
5. **R6:** it is hummed through the letterbox, slowed and a semitone flat, with a chalk scrape on each beat.
6. **R7:** her last "I swear, I swe-" is stretched and wrong, and Mom's "He said no." cuts it off. Silence.
7. **Healed, at dawn:** Lina sings new words to the same tune: "Me and you, and others too. Go wherever. I'll still care."

## The seven rules (verbatim)

Written by Danny's mom in purple ink on an index card. At the top is one name, scribbled out in blue pen.

1. You don't have to answer after goodnight. Phone off. Sleep.
2. A gift is not a bill. If a present makes you feel you owe someone, give it back.
3. Your password is yours. Your location is for your parents only.
4. If someone keeps turning up where you are, tell a grown-up. Never go alone to sort it out.
5. A real friend is happy you have other people. Never let anyone pull you away from your family.
6. If someone says their feelings are your fault, they're not. You're allowed to leave.
7. Don't keep it secret. Tell a grown-up. Save the messages. If they don't listen, tell another. Keep telling.

Adam keeps rule 1 and breaks rules 2 to 5. He nearly breaks rule 6: he comes two steps down toward the chalk, and Lina's reveal stops him. He keeps rule 7.

Every rule reads true for both boys: for Adam against Evie, and first for Danny against Adam.

## Beat outline

Writer's notes:
- No dialogue tags. Each quote is its own sentence next to an action.
- Every narrated line must be drawable.
- Day labels go on screen in a small chalk font.

### COLD OPEN (about 65 words)

Black. Four chalk taps: the motif rhythm with no tune.

- **"If someone you met three days ago says you're their favourite person in the whole world... don't say it back."**
  - Screen: a phone in a dark room showing the message "you're my favourite person in the whole world". A gap-tooth smile in the phone's glow, just out of focus.
- **"If a friend says real friends share passwords... don't type yours in."**
  - Screen: a password field and a thumb hovering. On a map, a white dot appears beside a blue one.
- **"And if someone is standing at a blue front door at midnight, smiling up at a dark window, two words chalked at their feet... don't go down. Wake a grown-up."**
  - Screen: OUR poster shot. Red wash, a BLUE front door, a small hooded silhouette seen from behind and standing perfectly still. ONLY ME is chalked at its feet, with curly M's that can be read. An upstairs curtain moves.
  - Draw the figure's sleeves chalk-white to the elbow and hide its face. The viewer assumes it is the girl.
- **"I know who it is."** Cut to black.

Seeds: T6 (the blue door, the sleeves, "I know who it is"), T3 (curly M's).

### HOOK (about 70 words)

- "When I was twelve, my best friend handed me seven rules on a card. His mom's purple handwriting. At the top, one name, scribbled out in blue pen. I never asked whose."
- "That week, a new girl decided I was hers. By Saturday midnight she was on our doormat, my little sister was reading a name under the landing light... and I found out who those rules were really for."

On screen:
- The card: purple lines and a blue scribble.
- Evie's eye at the letterbox slot, then Lina on the landing holding the card up to the bulb.

Seeds: T4, T7.

**S2, the legend.** The playground at sunset, wide.

**S3, Monday breakfast.**

### RULE 1 (about 150 words)

**"Rule number one. You don't have to answer after goodnight. Phone off. Sleep."**

- **Wednesday night.** Ping, ping, ping. Each ping is one note of the rhyme (motif #2).
  - The counter climbs: 12, 30, 47.
  - Evie whispers a few: "Are you awake?" "Goodnight!" "Why aren't you answering?"
- Adam KEEPS the rule. He turns the phone off and puts it face down.
- **The dead screen lights up anyway.** Evie: "I can see your light's still on."
- He goes to the window. At the end of the path, on the pavement under the streetlight, Evie is smiling up in phone glow. ONLY ME is chalked at her feet. She doesn't wave, and she doesn't blink.
- He switches the light off. When he looks again, she has gone flat on the pavement like a chalk drawing and slides away. She's gone.
- **Thursday morning.**
  - The path is wet and clean. Narration: "Must have rained."
  - A bucket stands by the door.
  - Mom is on the phone, with red knuckles. Mom: "I know. I'll talk to him. Thank you for telling me." (seeds T4, T5)

Lesson: point 1 (nonstop messages are a warning sign) and point 4 (you never owe anyone an answer).

### COMMENT PROMPT (about 35 words)

"Forty-seven messages, in one night. Before rule two, one word in the comments. If someone did that for you... would you feel Special, or Trapped?"

### RULE 2 (about 130 words)

**"Rule number two. A gift is not a bill. If a present makes you feel you owe someone, give it back."**

- **Thursday, the lockers.** Inside his locker, a woven green-and-gold bracelet with chalk dust on it.
- Evie is suddenly one inch from his shoulder, never touching. Evie: "I made it myself. For you. Only me?"
- **Lands T1:** "It was the bracelet I made Danny." Adam decides Danny gave it to her, or that his new friends sent her. He glares across at Danny's table.
- He keeps it (BREAKS the rule). It sits heavy in his pocket. Narration: "Now I owed her."
- **That night:** ONLY ME is halfway up the path, and she is standing in it. For the first time her chalk smile runs past the corners of her mouth.

Lesson: point 1 (gifts that make you feel you owe someone) and point 3 (nobody can make you owe them).

### RULE 3 (about 170 words)

**"Rule number three. Your password is yours. Your location is for your parents only."**

- **Thursday lunch.** Evie slides her phone across the table to him (no touching). Evie: "Best friends share passwords. Danny never trusted you. Don't you trust me?"
- He types his password in (BREAKS the rule). On his map, a white dot joins his blue one.
- Her dot gets everywhere before him: the bus stop, the corner shop, the end of Danny's street.
  - Narration: "Every place her dot waited was somewhere I used to wait." (seeds T6)
- **2 a.m.** Her dot is on his house. Then inside it. Then in his room.
  - He shines a torch: nobody.
  - Just one white chalk fingerprint, on the INSIDE of his window glass.
  - Outside, ONLY ME is at the foot of the step.
- **Friday morning.** Mute icons sit next to Mom, Lina and Danny on his phone. Evie: "Now it's just us."
- In the corridor, Mrs. Rahman at her door. Mrs. Rahman: "Just checking in, Adam. My door's open." He walks past. (seeds T4, T5, ending)

Lesson: point 1 and point 4. Someone with your password can cut you off from everyone, and your location goes to your parents only.

### RULE 4 (about 220 words)

**"Rule number four. If someone keeps turning up where you are, tell a grown-up. Never go alone to sort it out."**

- **Break time.** Evie sits on the swings and sings his rhyme, every word perfect (motif #3).

- Danny, blank: "What song?"
- **Lands T2:** He doesn't even remember it. "She hadn't learned it from Danny. Only one person still knew that song."
- **Sunset.** Adam goes ALONE to the Keeping Square to sort it out (BREAKS the rule).
- **The square:** chalk in his own handwriting with curly M's. DANNY has been rubbed out and ADAM written over it. Under it: ONLY ME.
  - The rhyme rises out of the square in his own seven-year-old voice, slowed (motif #4).
- **Lands T3:** "I told you nobody was dumb enough to try it. I was. Sunday. Sunset. I chalked Danny's name."
- The chalk lines on the playground slide together, lie flat, and stand up into Evie (CREEPY form, red wash).
  - She blocks the gate with her arms spread, one inch away, never touching.
  - Evie: "You asked for someone who'd never leave. Here I am."
- Narration (graft from B): "Someone who can never leave isn't a friend. They're a prisoner. And the square doesn't change the name you write. It sends you someone who holds on exactly as tight as you do."

- **That night:** ONLY ME is on the step.

Lesson:
- Point 1: turning up uninvited is a warning sign.
- Point 5: never go alone to sort it out.
- Point 3: you cannot wish someone into liking you, and trying hurt a real person.

### RULE 5 (about 150 words)

**"Rule number five. A real friend is happy you have other people. Never let anyone pull you away from your family."**

- **Saturday dinner.** Mom, Lina and Adam at the table.
- Behind Mom, OUTSIDE the dark window (seen through the glass, not reflected), Evie mouths the words as his phone buzzes. Evie's voice on the messages:
  - "Your mom's always on the phone about you. She thinks something's wrong with you."
  - "Your sister's a baby. Only I get you."

- A knock on his door: Lina, with the drawing. He shuts the door.

- Outside, Evie's neck bends up, and up, toward his window. Her smile is almost at her ears.

Lesson: point 1 (running down your family is control) and point 2 (real friends are glad you have other people).

### RULE 6 (about 160 words)

**"Rule number six. If someone says their feelings are your fault, they're not. You're allowed to leave."**

- **Midnight, red wash.** From the landing he hears the letterbox lift.
- Evie's chalk-white fingers come through, far too long, and slide along the hall floor toward the stairs, writing O-N-L-Y as they go.
- Through the slot, her smile runs ear to ear. The rhyme is hummed slowly, a semitone flat, with a chalk scrape on every beat (motif #5).

- The chalk stub drops through the slot and rolls to the bottom stair.
- Evie: "Write your name. Under mine."

- Lina: "It says your name."
- **Lands T4:** "The rules weren't for me. Danny's mom wrote them to protect Danny. From me."
  - Quick flashes: Danny's bare wrist. His mom looking away. Mom on the phone: "Thank you for telling me." Mrs. Rahman's open door.
- "And the words coming through our letterbox... I knew them."

Lesson:
- Point 1: guilt trips are a warning sign, and other people's feelings are not your job.
- The turn toward point 6: he was the one who couldn't let go.

### RULE 7 and CLIMAX (about 250 words)

**"Rule number seven. Don't keep it secret. Tell a grown-up. Save the messages. If they don't listen, tell another. Keep telling."**

- He steps over the chalk stub without touching it. The kitchen light is already on.
  - Mom is up, in her coat, with the bucket by the door and red knuckles.
- He tells her everything: the square, Danny, the bracelet, the password, Evie.
- Mom: "Thank you for telling me."
  - Narration: "Every morning she'd said that on the phone. To Danny's mom. Now she said it to me."
- **Lands T5:** "It never rained. Every dawn, Mom had scrubbed my handwriting off our path. She'd called Mrs. Rahman. And she'd waited. For me to let her in."
- Mom screenshots every one of Evie's messages. Then she scrolls up... and stops.
  - It is the old thread with Danny: the same words, word for word. "After everything I gave you." "If you leave, it's your fault."
  - "Forty-seven. In one night. A normal amount."
  - At the top, Danny's last message: "Please stop. You're scaring me."
- **Lands T6, gut-punch 1:** the cold open replays, wider.
  - The door is BLUE. It is Danny's door.
  - The still figure with chalk-white sleeves is Adam, on Sunday night, with ONLY ME in his own curly M's.
  - Upstairs, Danny's mom stands at the window with a phone to her ear.
  - "Danny didn't go down. He woke his mom. Just like the rule says."
- **At the front door.** Mom opens it and stands at his shoulder. The red wash stops at her shoulder.
  - Evie is on the doormat, her smile at full width. Evie: "Write your name?"
  - Adam: "No. I'm not your only. And Danny was never mine."
  - Then, kindly. Adam: "I hope you find lots of people."
  - Mom: "He said no."
- The rhyme breaks off at "I swear, I swe-" (motif #6). Evie's colour drains to chalk-white. The night wind lifts her away as dust, down the path toward the school. Silence.
- **Dawn.** Mom, Adam and Lina scrub the path together. Adam scrubs ONLY off the hall floor himself.
  - Lina, scrubbing, sings: "Me and you, and others too. Go wherever. I'll still care." (motif healed)

Lesson:
- Point 5: tell, save the messages, keep telling. It is never your fault.
- Point 7: you can be kind and still say no.
- Point 2: caring is not owning.

### ENDING (about 210 words)

- **Monday, Mrs. Rahman's office.**
  - Adam: "I think I'm the one who couldn't let go."
  - Mrs. Rahman: "That was brave. Caring isn't owning. We'll work on it together."
- With Mom beside him, he sends Danny one message: "Sorry. I'll give you space. You don't have to answer."
  - No reply, and he doesn't send another.
  - Narration: "The hardest rule. Nobody had to write that one down."
- **Clean-up.**
  - The bracelet in his drawer is a little heap of chalk dust.
  - Evie's desk is empty, and there is no Evie in the register.
  - The caretaker paints over the Keeping Square in fresh bright paint.
- **Weeks later, the lunch hall.** Danny, Kofi and Sam stop at his table.
  - Danny: "Can we sit here? All of us?"
  - Adam: "Yeah. All of you."
  - Danny slides a shoebox across: every gift Adam ever gave him, including the REAL green-and-gold bracelet.
  - The card lies on the table. The rules are in his mom's purple; the scribble is in Danny's chewed blue pen.
  - **Lands T7, gut-punch 2.** Danny: "I crossed it out. You were still my friend. I just needed room."
- Narration: "He's not my only friend. He's one of my best. That's better."
- **CODA.** The grown narrator; only his hands are drawn.
  - "Last week, Danny didn't answer all day. My fingertips went white. That old pull. I put my phone down."
  - Morning: one chalk word on his path, ONLY. His hands scrub it off with a bucket, "the way Mom did."
  - He calls Danny once, and Danny picks up on the first ring. "He always does. I only call once."

Lesson: point 6 (if you are the one who can't let go, talk to a grown-up; asking for help is brave) and point 2 (real care gives space).

### OUTRO (about 185 words)

"Okay. Breathe. You can turn a light on now. None of this happened. No Keeping Square, no Evie, no chalk on the path. The monster is made up. The warning signs are real."

**On-screen checklist (6):**
1. **Too much, too fast:** "favourite person ever" within days, nonstop messages, gifts that make you feel you owe them.
2. **"Only me":** gets upset when you see friends or family, says bad things about them, turns up where you are uninvited.
3. **Your passwords are yours.** Your location is for your parents only.
4. **"It's your fault" is never true.** You can say no, not answer, block and report. You can be kind and still say no.
5. **Don't keep it secret.** Tell a grown-up, save the messages, and keep telling until someone helps. It's never your fault.
6. **Can't stop checking on someone, or can't let them go?** That isn't caring; it's holding on. Tell a grown-up you trust. Asking for help is brave.

Then: "You can't make anyone like you. And nobody can make you owe them."

**Comment payoff:** "Now. Special, or Trapped? Those forty-seven messages were mine first. To Danny. If you said Special, that's what I thought I was giving him. If you said Trapped, that's what he felt. Same messages. Reply to your own comment with the one word that fixes both: Space."

**Like and subscribe:** "Leave a like if you've ever been a Danny... or an Adam. And subscribe. On Night Policy, you can leave whenever you want. That's how you know we're real friends."

**Last line:** "Sleep well. And if anyone ever promises they'll never leave you... ask them what happens when you say no."

No "Next time" teaser.

## Safety and originality check

**Halal and kid-safe:**
- No romance. Evie's fixation is "best friends, only me", the boy Adam held on to is a best friend, and the words crush, girlfriend and love-talk never appear.
- Nobody touches anybody: she blocks, reaches and stands too close, and the chalk stub is dropped, never handed over.
- Everyone dresses modestly.
- Chalk takes the place of blood and claws. There are no injuries, weapons or deaths: she drains to dust and blows away.
- The wish is the evil thing, and it visibly hurt Danny and Adam's family. The fix is the real-world one: tell Mom, save the messages, the counsellor, a kind clear no, and giving Danny space.
- Adam is helped, not shamed. Parents and helpers are loving and competent, and the counsellor wears a hijab.

**Reference film:** No shop-bought wish toy, no music shop, no workplace romance, none of its names, no blood, and none of its key phrase. We keep only the feeling: the too-wide smile, a still figure at a door at night, red nights, and marks on the ground.

**Our earlier films:**
- The rules come from a friend's mom, and the monster never dictates them.
- Nothing fades, there are no reflections, and the chalk is words she stands on, never steps (Mirror Steps).
- None of these: a body swap, a parent copy, a voice on the phone, a guardian neighbour, an empty seat, a list of names, a code word, a wall-dweller, a chat avatar, a jar, eye colour, an invisible boy, a narrator-job twist, or a threatened child of the narrator.
- Mom never explains the signs away.
