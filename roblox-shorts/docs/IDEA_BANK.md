# Idea bank: 16 original "In this world…" ideas

Companion to `docs/SCRIPT_FORMULA.md`. Every idea is built to fit the 2:15 beat sheet:
- the rule on frame 1;
- society, then the rule within the rule;
- "I" by 0:23;
- twist 1 at about 0:30;
- twist 2 at 1:20-1:40;
- a choice made by gesture;
- a time-jump ending.

**What we avoided:**
- **Everything in the five references:** creatures that take kids at night, a sacrifice on the porch, first-word powers, tooth fairies, height gates, shadows, music boxes, scarecrows, time rewinds.
- **The most common viral rules:** moving is deadly, soulmate strings or timers, death countdowns, lies that change your body, mirror doubles, "the floor is lava", imaginary friends retiring, monsters under the bed, toys that come alive.
- **Not yet checked:** no web search was run for this list. Before scripting any idea, search YouTube Shorts and TikTok for its rule phrase and drop or reshape it if something close exists.

**Scoring.** Each idea gets 1-10 on eight criteria, for a total out of 80:
- **Hook:** the first sentence.
- **Curiosity:** the curiosity gap.
- **Twist:** twist power.
- **Emotion:** emotional payoff.
- **Original:** originality.
- **Visual:** visual potential.
- **Appeal:** kid + teen + adult appeal.
- **Ease:** ease of animating. 10 means few characters, simple sets and stock Roblox tricks.

Ties are broken by Emotion, then Original, then Visual.

**TF** marks the ideas that share the owner's favourite's (r4 Tooth Fairies) qualities:
- a childhood myth, ritual, saying or everyday object secretly working as something bigger;
- wonder plus a little creepiness;
- a heart-breaking choice between keeping and letting go.

11 of the 16 qualify, and 4 more partly.

## Shortlist: the top 2

### 1. Wind Change (72/80)

**Logline.** In a town where a face pulled during a wind change stays forever, a ten-year-old has spent three years inventing silly faces for a mother who has been stuck crying since the day of Grandpa's funeral. His plan is to climb the hill at the 3:15 wind change and get his own face stuck funny, so she can laugh at it every day. His face won't stick. Instead the faceless wind carries him to the church steeple, where it keeps every face it ever took, his mother's laugh among them. There he learns that the crying face she has worn for three years is his own: on that day, when he was seven, she traded her laugh to give him his face back. To give her laugh back, he has to take his tears back, forever.

**Title options:** *Don't Pull That Face* (a forbidden act, recommended) / *Wind Change* / *The Wind Takes Faces*.

**Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, if the wind changes while you're pulling a face, it stays like that. Forever." (17 words; the punch word is "Forever")
- *Frame 1:* a boy on a hilltop pulling a silly face, autumn leaves whipping round. The weathervane squeals and spins, and his face decal locks with a *click*.
- *Caption:* FOREVER.

**Hidden secret.** The wind is a creature, the Gust:
- **Body:** tall, made of swirling leaves on a rusty weathervane spine.
- **Face:** none. A smooth empty oval where a face should be.
- **Why it takes faces:** it has no face of its own, so it takes the one you were making. The original goes up to its roost in the church steeple: thousands of faces, still moving, like masks that never stop.
- **The rules it keeps:**
  - It never gives a face back. It only *trades*: one face for another, given on purpose.
  - It takes only one face from anyone, ever.

**Society (consequence block):**
- Wind-change sirens, and forecasts that give the minute ("wind change at 3:15, keep a straight face").
- School photos are only taken indoors.
- A teenager is stuck with duck lips.
- The "grumpy" baker isn't grumpy; he has been stuck mid-sneeze since 1987.
- Dark note: nobody ever cries outside.
- Visual full stop: at the siren, every shutter on the street closes.

**Rule within the rule.**
- Line: "The wind never gives a face back. It only trades. Yours for theirs. Nobody's ever been that brave."
- **Lock:** it never gives back.
- **Key:** a willing trade.
- A second clause is never spoken but is shown as a clue later: one face per person, ever.

**One person + plant.**
- The narrator is 10. Mom's face has been stuck crying for three years, since the windy day of Grandpa's funeral. She cries at his birthday, at cartoons, at good news.
- "I don't remember the funeral. Mom says I slept through it." This is her lie, and it is the clue.
- He has invented 47 silly faces and named every one: the Frog, the Melted Snowman, the Grandpa Sneeze.
- Visual: a chart on his bedroom wall, "47 FACES — LAUGHS: 0". She claps every night; her face can't laugh.
- **Visual-only plant:** a fridge photo of the funeral day. He is 7, his face twisted crying in the wind. It is never mentioned.

**Twist 1 (~0:30).** Kid logic: "If my face got stuck funny, she could laugh every day." At 3:15 he climbs the hill and pulls the Melted Snowman. "The wind changed. And my face didn't stick." *(pause)* The wind stops dead. Something with no face bends down and looks at him.

**Middle.**
- **Main reveal:** the Gust carries him to the steeple. Walls of faces: the baker's sneeze, the duck lips… and right in the middle, still laughing, his mom.
- **Reframe line:** "It wasn't stealing faces. It was trying them on."
- **Dilemma:** he offers the trade. "No more Frog. No more Snowman. No more me." The Gust shakes its empty head.
- **Clock:** the weathervane is swinging back; the wind change lasts one minute.
- **Attempts:** he pulls every face he has, in a compressed run: "Frog. No. Snowman. No. Sneeze. No." Then: "By face 47, the weathervane was almost home." Why won't it take him?

**Twist 2 (~1:25).**
- From the very top of the roost the Gust lifts down one small crying face. "I knew that face. It was on our fridge." It is him, at seven.
- "The wind only takes one face from anyone. It already had mine."
- Mom never got stuck. On the funeral day *his* face stuck crying, and she traded her laugh to give him his face back.
- The face she has worn for three years is his.
- "She wasn't crying for Grandpa. She was crying my tears. So I wouldn't have to." *(pause)*

**Emotional ending.**
- **Choice by gesture:** "I looked at her laugh, then at my old face, and I nodded." *Click.*
- **Spectacle:** a gust across town, every stuck face flickers, and at home the loudest laugh on the street. The neighbours open their shutters.
- **Time jump:** "That was 20 years ago." His face still cries; he doesn't.
- **Generational mirror:** his daughter has 52 silly faces and does one for him every night. "My face can't laugh. Mom says it doesn't have to."
- **Optional sting:** "Last night, at 3:15, I found her on the hill."

**Why it is visual in Roblox 3D.**
- Face decals are Roblox's native acting tool, and the whole plot is decal swaps, so it costs almost nothing to produce.
- The Gust is a particle body on a weathervane mesh with an empty face.
- The steeple roost is a wall of floating, animated decals: a signature set and a thumbnail.
- Built-in in-joke: Roblox avatars really do have one face.
- The colour arc follows the clock: autumn gold, then a grey steeple, then a warm kitchen at sunset.

**Cast and sets:**
- **Cast:** the boy, Mom, the Gust, the baker, the duck-lips teen, and the daughter in the epilogue.
- **Sets:** a hilltop with a weathervane, the town street with a siren, the kitchen, the steeple roost, and the epilogue living room.

**Comment-proofing (one line each in the script):**
- Why didn't it take his face on the hill? One face per person, and it already had his.
- Why doesn't he remember? He was seven, and Mom said he slept through it.
- Why did nobody trade before? "Nobody's ever been that brave." Mom was.

### 2. Grandpa Gnome (69/80)

**Logline.** In a world where grandparents don't die but turn into garden gnomes, an eleven-year-old girl must leave her gnome grandpa behind when her family sells the house. The night before the move she finds him at the garden gate, facing the moving truck, trying to come with her. Only family can carry a gnome out of its garden. At midnight she learns what gnomes really do. The new family's six-year-old is already asleep in her old room, and the only thing standing between his window and what comes up through the lawn is the gnome in her arms.

**Title options:** *Grandpa Is A Gnome* (recommended) / *Keep Off The Lawn After Dark* / *Grandpa Gnome*.

**Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, grandparents don't die. They turn into garden gnomes." (11 words; the punch word is "gnomes")
- *Frame 1:* an old man dozing in a deck chair at dusk. A puff of dandelion seed, and in the chair sits a garden gnome with his exact moustache and fishing hat.
- *Caption:* GNOMES.

**Hidden secret.** Gnomes are not decorations. They are the fence.
- At midnight the lawns ripple, and the **Diggers** come up: mole-like mounds with glowing eyes that pull things down into the dirt (toys, pets, anything small).
- Every gnome lights a tiny lantern and holds its own garden till dawn.
- A gnome guards the garden it stands in, and whoever is small in that house.

**Society:**
- Families never move.
- House ads read "FOR SALE — COMES WITH ANCESTORS".
- Stealing a gnome is the worst crime in town.
- The Pikes put up plastic gnomes to look normal, and last spring their dog was gone, with a hole where the kennel stood. This is the mirror, and it pays in beat 9.
- The oldest gnome on the street is great-great-grandma Edie, since 1902, with no nose.
- Dark note and sign: "KEEP OFF THE LAWN AFTER DARK."

**Rule within the rule.**
- Line: "A gnome can never leave its garden on its own. Only family can carry it out."
- **Lock:** he can't leave by himself.
- **Key:** her arms. The choice is entirely hers.

**One person + plant.**
- The narrator is 11. "Grandpa turned last spring. Fishing hat and all." She still says goodnight to him, and he blinks once; only she sees it.
- **Plant:** when he was alive, every night at nine, he walked the lawn with a flashlight "looking for moles". The family laughed at him.
- Dad lost his job and the house is sold.
- At the viewing, the new family's boy Theo (6) asked if the gnome was nice.

**Twist 1 (~0:30).** "Then, the night before we moved, Grandpa was gone." She finds him at the very edge of the garden, at the gate, facing the moving truck. He walked there, and he can't go one step further. "He was trying to come with me." *(pause)*

**Middle.**
- **Choice made:** she picks him up; only family can.
- **Midnight, main reveal:**
  - the lawn ripples;
  - dirt mounds rise with glowing eyes;
  - all down the street, gnomes light their lanterns;
  - at the Pikes', a plastic gnome is pulled under like it's nothing.
- **Reframe line:** "Gnomes aren't decorations. They're the fence."
- **Dilemma:** Theo's family arrived early, and he's asleep in her old room. If she carries Grandpa away, nothing watches that window. "No more goodnights. No more blink. No more Grandpa."
- **Attempt:** she plants Mom's plastic gnome from the truck under Theo's window. It is dragged under in a second. The mounds move closer to his wall.

**Twist 2 (~1:25).**
- In her arms, Grandpa turns his head, away from her and the truck, toward Theo's window, and his lantern lights.
- The plant pays: "He was never looking for moles. Every night at nine, for eleven years, he was doing this."
- "He wasn't guarding our house. He was guarding whoever was small in it. And that wasn't me anymore." *(pause)*

**Emotional ending.**
- **Choice by gesture:** "I knelt down, put him under Theo's window, and let go." His lantern flares and the Diggers sink.
- At dawn she teaches Theo the goodnight and the blink.
- **Spectacle:** from the truck's back window, a line of lanterns down the street, one blinking.
- **Time jump and narrator reveal:** "That was 60 years ago." The kid voice belongs to an old woman. Every spring Theo, grown up, sends a photo: Grandpa, still blinking.
- **Last line:** "Last week, my granddaughter said I'm getting shorter. I already know whose garden I'm going to."

**Why it is visual in Roblox 3D.**
- Gnomes are perfect cute-creepy props: simple meshes with Grandpa's decal and hat.
- A moonlit garden street lit by dozens of tiny lanterns is the signature shot and the thumbnail.
- The Diggers are just dirt mounds plus glowing eyes, so they are cheap and scary.
- The colour arc runs dusk, then midnight blue, then dawn. A moving truck and a FOR SALE sign carry the plot with no extra words.

**Cast and sets:**
- **Cast:** the girl (old in the epilogue), Grandpa (human in flashback, then a gnome), Mom, Dad, Theo, the Diggers, and street gnomes including Edie.
- **Sets:** the garden and street at dusk and at night, the moving truck, Theo's window, and the epilogue garden.

**Comment-proofing:**
- Why not take Grandpa and buy a new gnome? The plastic one fails on screen.
- Why does Grandpa go to the gate? He loved her. He turned back when he saw who needed him.
- What if there's no garden? The family is moving to a flat. That is the whole problem.

### 3. Bless You (TF, 67/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, every sneeze knocks your soul out. Only a 'bless you' puts it back." *Frame 1:* a kid sneezes, a see-through copy of him pops out a foot, Mom says "Bless you" and it snaps back.
- **Secret:** a soul nobody blesses within three seconds floats up into **the Drift**, a slow river of souls above the roofs where the dead go too. The body stays behind, quiet and forgetful; the town calls it "going vague".
- **Society:**
  - Nobody sneezes alone, and schools pair "bless-you buddies".
  - Pepper is sold only at the pharmacy, with ID.
  - In hay-fever season people walk in twos.
  - "A good neighbour can hear you sneeze through the wall."
  - Every spring, a few people go vague.
- **Lock / key:** you can't bless yourself / anyone who can see your soul can bless it, within three seconds.
- **Person + plant:**
  - A girl of 10 lives with Grandma, who went vague three years ago, the week after Grandpa's funeral.
  - Grandma had the loudest sneeze on the street and taught her three ways to say it: "Bless you. Gesundheit. Salud."
  - **Visual-only plant:** a pepper shaker on the attic windowsill.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** "Then last night, I sneezed alone in the attic." Nobody said it. She floats out, sees her own body on the floor, and above the roof the Drift, with Grandma in it.
- **Middle:**
  - She swims the Drift: a 1920s farmer, a vague dog, souls of every age.
  - Her body's fingers start to fade, which is the clock.
  - She reaches Grandma, who is holding hands with Grandpa.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** the pepper shaker pays. Grandma sneezed alone on purpose, the week after the funeral. "She didn't go vague. She went to find him." The narrator has three seconds and three ways to say it.
- **Ending:**
  - She doesn't say it.
  - Grandma, silent for three years, says "Bless you" to *her*, and she drops back into her body.
  - Downstairs, Grandma's body smiles once and sleeps (off-screen, gentle).
  - Time jump: "That was 20 years ago. Last night my son sneezed. I said bless you. And from somewhere above the roof, two old voices said it too."
- **Roblox visuals:** translucent soul avatars (a transparency slider), the Drift as a glowing ribbon in a violet sky, pepper behind pharmacy glass, BLESS-YOU BUDDY posters, an attic.
- **Risks:** death and dementia themes. Keep it loving and never show a death. "Going vague" must not mock real illness.

### 4. Kissed Better (TF, 66/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, when a parent kisses your scrape better, it heals. Because it moves to them." *Frame 1:* Dad kisses a scraped knee, a tiny red bug-like **ouch** crawls onto his lip, and the knee is clean.
- **Secret:** hurts are little red creatures. A kiss moves them into the kisser, forever. A forehead kiss moves the big ones: fear, nightmares, sadness.
- **Society:**
  - Parents wear long sleeves all summer.
  - Grown-ups limp from their kids' bike crashes.
  - "Don't run, your mom only has two knees."
  - Parents who won't kiss are whispered about.
  - Grandparents carry three generations of ouches.
- **Lock / key:** an ouch can never be kissed back / it only goes home if you ask for it.
- **Person + plant:**
  - A boy of 9, the clumsiest kid in town. Dad has been single since Mom left four years ago.
  - Dad kisses everything better, plus a forehead kiss every night "for bad dreams".
  - "Dad has kissed 312 ouches better. I counted." The list: a skinned knee, a bee sting, a broken wrist.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** Dad falls asleep on the couch with his sleeve rolled up. Every scrape the boy ever had is on Dad's arm, and Dad's crooked wrist is why he can't hold a pen.
- **Middle:** he tries never to get hurt again (he hides a sprained ankle). Dad is going grey, literally losing his colour and sitting in the dark.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** the forehead kisses. Every night since Mom left, Dad has taken the boy's sadness. That is why he never cried about her, and why Dad is turning grey. "He wasn't kissing me goodnight. He was carrying it."
- **Ending:**
  - He holds Dad's face and asks for "the Mom one" back.
  - He cries for the first time in four years, and colour floods back into Dad.
  - 20 years later his son falls. He kisses the knee, feels the sting, and smiles.
  - Last line: "Then my son kissed my hand. And it stopped hurting."
- **Roblox visuals:** ouch-bugs (simple glowing meshes), bandages, Dad desaturating shot by shot, home sets only.
- **Risks:** same engine as ideas 5 and 9.

### 5. Two Umbrellas (partly TF, 65/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, it only rains on people who are sad." *Frame 1:* a sunny street, and one kid under a small cloud raining only on him.
- **Secret:** an umbrella doesn't stop the rain; it moves the rain to whoever holds it. And you can never hold your own.
- **Society:**
  - You can't hide sadness; weather reports are mood reports.
  - Funerals flood the street.
  - Schools hire umbrella monitors, and the rich pay umbrella-holders.
  - Lonely people are always wet.
  - "A friend is someone who holds your umbrella."
- **Lock / key:** your own umbrella flips inside out / two people can hold each other's.
- **Person + plant:**
  - A girl of 13, sad since her parents split.
  - Her best friend Sam has held her umbrella every day for a year and is "the driest kid in school".
  - **Visual-only plants:** Sam's damp hoodie, his squelching sneakers, his sneezes.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** behind the gym she sees Sam alone, with no umbrella, under a cloud the size of a house. He was never dry.
- **Middle:** guilt; her own umbrella flips; her bedroom floods; Sam's cloud grows lightning.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** Sam's cloud isn't his. It is a year of *her* rain, and nobody holds his umbrella for his own. "He wasn't the driest kid in school. He was carrying two clouds."
- **Ending:**
  - She takes her rain back and holds Sam's umbrella while he holds hers. Both are wet, both are okay. "It still rained. It was just rain now."
  - 20 years later her daughter gets her first cloud. She lifts an umbrella, and her daughter grabs the other handle.
- **Roblox visuals:** personal rain clouds are a one-click particle effect; puddles, umbrellas, and bright skies against grey ones.
- **Risks:** a raincloud over a sad person is a known cartoon gag, so the umbrella mechanism has to carry the originality. Keep the depression theme hopeful.

### 6. Picked Last (65/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, whoever gets picked last for a team is forgotten by morning." *Frame 1:* a gym, two teams, one kid alone on the line; overnight his name fades from the class list.
- **Secret:** the forgotten are still here, unseen and unheard, living in the school after dark: a team of kids from 1985, 1999 and 2012. Only the captain who left you can remember you.
- **Society:**
  - Kids train all summer so they won't be last.
  - Captains are bribed with lunch.
  - Teachers are not allowed to pick.
  - Every class photo has a gap.
  - **Visual-only:** the narrator's family sets four plates for three people.
- **Lock / key:** only the captain who left you remembers you / a captain who picks a forgotten kid first brings them back, but then someone else must be last.
- **Person + plant:** a boy of 12, captain for the first time. His best friend Arlo is the slowest kid in school. The fourth plate is the plant.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** down to two, Arlo and the new girl. He picks Arlo. The next morning her desk is gone, and he is the only one who remembers her.
- **Middle:** he follows her voice into the gym at night and finds the forgotten team. Among them is a boy who looks like him.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** it is his older brother, picked last six years ago: the fourth plate. "Mom forgot him. Her hands didn't."
- **Ending:**
  - He picks his brother first, then the new girl.
  - He hands the captain's whistle to Arlo and steps into the line himself.
  - Time jump: "My brother still sets one plate too many. He doesn't know why. I do." The narrator is speaking from the gym.
  - Warmer alternative: the loophole "there's only a last if someone's left", so he picks everyone at once.
- **Roblox visuals:** a night gym full of kids in different decades' outfits (great avatar variety), names fading on a list, the extra plate. Low creature cost.
- **Risks:** the dark ending (offer the warm variant); it is less magical to look at.

### 7. The Lost Room (TF, 64/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, nothing is ever really lost. It all goes to one room." *Frame 1:* a red balloon slips from a hand, then a vast room where it floats in among thousands.
- **Secret:** the **Keeper**, a mole with a hundred pockets and spectacles, keeps everything anyone loses: socks, keys, mittens, balloons, and lost memories in labelled jars. You can claim a thing back only by trading something you'd miss.
- **Society:**
  - Every school lost-and-found box is a door to the room, and claim tickets are treasured.
  - "Losers weepers" is a crime.
  - Old people queue at the boxes.
  - A man who lost his temper in 1999 still hasn't claimed it.
- **Lock / key:** nothing leaves unless something you'd miss goes in / unless whoever lost it gives it away.
- **Person + plant:**
  - A girl of 11 whose Grandpa, who taught her chess, forgets her name some days.
  - **Plant:** three empty jam jars by his bed. He always asks for "a fresh jar".
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** she climbs into the school's lost-and-found box, falls into the Room, and finds a whole shelf labelled with Grandpa's name.
- **Middle:** she tries to steal his jars back. The Keeper stops her. She offers her bike, then her chess set.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** Grandpa isn't losing them. He brings them here himself, every night, in jam jars, and every jar is labelled with *her* name: his memories of her, stored where they can never be lost. "He wasn't forgetting me. He was keeping me safe."
- **Ending:**
  - She leaves the jars and takes one claim ticket.
  - 20 years later she brings her daughter through the box. Beside Grandpa's shelf are new jars in her own handwriting, labelled with her daughter's name.
- **Roblox visuals:** an endless warehouse of lost things (a balloon ceiling, sock mountains, an umbrella forest), glowing jars that play scenes, a cute-creepy mole.
- **Risks:** memory jars are a known image; dementia must be handled with love.

### 8. Twenty-Year Echo (partly TF, 64/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, every echo comes back. Just not for twenty years." *Frame 1:* a kid shouts into a canyon; silence; caption "20".
- **Secret:** the canyon, a giant stone face called **the Listener**, keeps every shout and gives it back 20 years later. And it runs both ways: what you shout today is heard 20 years ago.
- **Society:**
  - People shout messages to their future selves.
  - Couples shout their vows, to hear them back at their 20th anniversary.
  - On Echo Sunday the town hears people long gone.
  - Swearing at the canyon is banned, because it comes back at your kid's graduation.
- **Lock / key:** the canyon returns only what was really shouted / it answers whoever stands where the shouter stood.
- **Person + plant:** a girl of 12 whose dad died before she can remember. Every Sunday she hears teenage Dad's echoes ("PIZZA!", "I'm gonna be famous!"). She has 300 voice memos on her old phone.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** this Sunday an echo says her name: Dad at 16, twenty years ago, before she existed.
- **Middle:** she shouts back, and the next Sunday he answers. A conversation at one line a week, told as a compressed montage.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** he says he'll name his daughter after "the voice in the canyon". "He didn't name me. I did." And she knows about the rainy night on the canyon road.
- **Ending:** warning him might mean she never exists. She shouts "Drive slow on the bridge. I love you." The next Sunday, an echo in a voice older than she has ever heard: "Thanks, kiddo." Cut.
- **Roblox visuals:** the Listener's stone face, travelling sound rings, split screens of teen Dad in 2000s clothes and her.
- **Risks:** the paradox may confuse young viewers; it is close to "messages to the past" films.

### 9. Heads Up (TF, 63/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, a penny you find heads up really gives you a whole day of luck." *Frame 1:* a glinting penny on a step; a kid picks it up and gold sparkles fall on him.
- **Secret:** luck is never made. Every lucky penny is a day of luck someone gave away, and they get the bad day instead.
- **Society:**
  - Penny hunts before school.
  - Casinos have penny detectors.
  - Strangers flip tails-up pennies over as a kindness.
  - Gamblers pay kids to find pennies.
  - "Never pick up a penny near the hospital."
- **Lock / key:** luck only works if you pick it up yourself / it goes back only if *they* pick it up.
- **Person + plant:**
  - A boy of 10, the luckiest kid in town: a penny heads up on his porch step every morning for five years.
  - He keeps them in a jar labelled "1,826".
  - Grandma next door has the worst luck in town.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** one morning there is no penny, and the day goes wrong in a fun chain of disasters.
- **Middle:** he waits up at 5 AM and sees Grandma in her nightgown, limping out to put down a penny. She has done it every day since his car accident at five.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** Grandma is in hospital with no luck left. "I was never lucky. I was given it."
- **Ending:**
  - He pours the whole jar into her hand and she closes her fingers. He is unlucky for a while, which is played for comedy.
  - 20 years later he leaves a penny on his daughter's step every morning.
  - Last line: "This morning, there were two."
- **Roblox visuals:** glinting pennies, gold luck sparkles against a tiny storm cloud over Grandma, a jar, a hospital corridor.
- **Risks:** same engine as ideas 4 and 5. It is less creepy than the owner's favourite.

### 10. The Moon Kid (TF, 62/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, the moon really does follow you home. But only one kid at a time." *Frame 1:* the back seat of a car; the moon slides sideways to keep pace.
- **Secret:** the moon chooses the loneliest kid in town and stays over them until someone needs it more.
- **Society:**
  - The Moon Kid can never hide, and the tide follows them (wet sneakers, flooded paths).
  - Tour buses come.
  - The last Moon Kid, in 1998, became a lighthouse keeper.
- **Lock / key:** the moon can't be told to leave / it leaves only when it is let go.
- **Person + plant:**
  - A girl of 12, the Moon Kid since the night her dad didn't come home.
  - She hates it: "Moon Girl", no sleep.
  - **Plant:** her three tricks for sleeping in moonlight (a mask, a song, a joke she tells the moon).
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** one night she's free. The moon follows the new boy across the street.
- **Middle:** she can't sleep without it, and the dark is too big. The boy's mom is in hospital.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** "It wasn't following me. It was keeping me company." It came the night Dad didn't come home.
- **Ending:**
  - She lets it go and gives the boy her three tricks.
  - 20 years later her daughter says, "Mom, the moon's following us." She doesn't look up. "Good. Wave at it."
- **Roblox visuals:** a huge moon with a subtle face, light-shaft spotlights, tide puddles following a kid, rooftops at night.
- **Risks:** the sky-and-letting-go tone sits closest to the owner's favourite. Keep its imagery different (no lift-off, no "tiny light").

### 11. The Last Egg (TF, 62/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, any Easter egg nobody finds by sunset hatches." *Frame 1:* a painted egg in tall grass at sunset; it cracks; something blinks inside.
- **Secret:** the eggs are laid by the Easter Bunny, an eight-foot, ragged, button-eyed rabbit, and they are her babies. The town's great hunt isn't a game. It is how the town keeps them from hatching.
- **Society:**
  - The hunt is a civic duty, with drones and sniffer dogs.
  - The Egg Count must match.
  - Since 1974, a sign: "BARN 7 — KEEP LOCKED".
  - Kids are ranked by finds.
- **Lock / key:** a hatched one can never be put back / it can only go home if a finder hands it over.
- **Person + plant:** a boy of 10, three-time hunt champion with no friends. He pockets egg number 500; he wants a pet.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** it hatches in his sock drawer: a fluffy thing with too many ears. The town's count is one short.
- **Middle:**
  - He hides "Button" from the drones.
  - Button grows every night and copies his voice, which is cute and creepy.
  - The town searches house to house.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** the Bunny comes to his window, not to attack but to tap softly. "We weren't hunting eggs. We were taking her babies."
- **Ending:**
  - He carries Button to the woods, and the Bunny lowers her ears.
  - 20 years later he "misses" one egg every year on purpose.
  - Last line: "This year, my daughter found it first."
- **Roblox visuals:** egg-hunt chaos, drones, a giant ragged rabbit, a fluffy creature that grows each scene.
- **Risks:** Easter-bunny horror exists online, and the idea is seasonal (best posted in March or April).

### 12. The Longest-Night Dinner (TF, 60/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, once a year, everyone who ever lived in your house comes back for dinner." *Frame 1:* a long candlelit table and guests in clothes from five different centuries.
- **Secret:** "everyone who ever lived here" includes the living. They come back at the age they were when they lived there.
- **Society:**
  - Families cook for forty.
  - House ads read "sleeps 4, dines 31".
  - New houses have lonely dinners.
  - Arguments with 1890s farmers about table manners.
- **Lock / key:** guests can't leave the table before dawn / they can hear only people who sit down with them.
- **Person + plant:** a girl of 12 who has moved with her mom into a 120-year-old farmhouse. Mom insisted on buying it and won't say why.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** at the first dinner, a 12-year-old girl with Mom's laugh: Mom grew up here.
- **Middle:** young Mom sits beside her dad, Grandpa at 40, laughing.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** Grandpa isn't dead. He is in a care home and no longer recognises Mom. This dinner is the only place her dad still knows her.
- **Ending:**
  - Next year they bring old Grandpa to the house. He sits across from his 40-year-old self and his 12-year-old daughter, and for one dinner he remembers.
  - 20 years later her daughter asks who the girl at the table is: "My mom. She's your age."
- **Roblox visuals:** a period-costume ensemble at one table, candlelight, snow outside, a farmhouse set.
- **Risks:** a large cast (keep the guests silent extras) and a gentle pace.

### 13. Don't Whistle (partly TF, 60/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, if you whistle after dark, something whistles back." *Frame 1:* a kid whistles on a dusk porch, and from the treeline the same notes come back.
- **Secret:** **the Whistler**, a thin creature of hollow reeds, learns any tune it hears after dark and uses it to call. It can only take someone who walks toward their own tune.
- **Society:**
  - Kettles are banned and trains use bells.
  - Night games use flags.
  - Grandmas sing a warning rhyme.
- **Lock / key:** it can only use a tune it was given / it stops calling if someone gives it a new song.
- **Person + plant:**
  - A boy of 10. Dad's three-note whistle calls him home at sunset, always before dark.
  - **Plant:** the boy has never been able to whistle, despite practising daily for years.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** five minutes after dark, Dad's three notes come from the woods, while Dad stands right beside him.
- **Middle:** the whistle comes closer every night, and Dad goes quiet.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** ten years ago Dad whistled after dark to find his lost toddler, and the Whistler led him there. Dad owes it. "It never stole Dad's tune. Dad gave it."
- **Ending:**
  - The boy finally whistles, a new song of his own, toward the woods. The Whistler only ever wanted a song.
  - Every sunset since, he whistles it a new one, and it whistles back the next day.
- **Roblox visuals:** a reed creature silhouette, dusk-to-dark colour, banned-kettle signs, a treeline.
- **Risks:** "don't whistle at night" exists as folklore and creepypasta, so originality is lower.

### 14. Hand-Me-Downs (TF, 59/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, if you wear hand-me-downs, you dream the dreams of everyone who wore them first." *Frame 1:* a kid falls asleep in an old yellow raincoat, and four dreams flicker above her.
- **Secret:** clothes keep their owners' dreams in the threads. The last dream of a missing owner keeps playing *live*.
- **Society:**
  - Thrift stores price clothes by dreams.
  - Rich kids wear 100-year-old sweaters.
  - Brand-new clothes are for people who want quiet nights.
  - A pilot's jacket in the museum is under guard.
- **Lock / key:** a dream plays only while the coat is worn / it ends when its first owner wears it again.
- **Person + plant:** the youngest of four sisters. Her raincoat's dreams: "Grandma dreamt of the sea. Mom dreamt of horses. June dreamt of falling."
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** a dream nobody claims. A boy on a boat calls "Rosie!", which is Grandma's name.
- **Middle:** Grandma admits the coat was first her little brother Tom's. He was lost at sea in 1958.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** the dream changes every night. Tom is alive, an old lighthouse keeper across the bay, still dreaming of his sister.
- **Ending:**
  - She puts the coat on Grandma's shoulders at the lighthouse door.
  - 20 years later her daughter wears the coat and dreams of two old people laughing on a boat.
- **Roblox visuals:** dream bubbles in different art eras, the yellow raincoat, a lighthouse, the sea.
- **Risks:** the reunion is weaker as a "choice". It needs a harder cost.

### 15. Never Wash It (TF, 59/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, your blankie catches every bad dream you have. That's why you never wash it." *Frame 1:* a ratty blanket twitching on a bed; something moves under the fabric.
- **Secret:** nightmares live in the fabric as creatures shaped like your fears. Washing frees them all at once, and blankies are handed down, so they hold fears from generations.
- **Society:**
  - Laundromats post "NO BLANKIES" signs.
  - A 40-year-old banker still carries his.
  - "Lost blankie" posters are scarier than "lost dog" ones.
- **Lock / key:** a freed nightmare stays real until sunrise / it goes back only if someone sleeps under the blanket with it.
- **Person + plant:** a boy of 11 who is embarrassed by his blankie before a sleepover. Mom puts it in the wash.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** the spin cycle. The door bursts and eleven years of nightmares crawl out: the faceless teacher, the stairs that never end, a spider wearing Grandma's glasses.
- **Middle:** a night-long catch. He knows each one's weakness because he dreamt it. Told as a compressed montage.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** the biggest, oldest nightmare isn't his. It is Dad's, from when the blankie was Dad's: losing his son.
- **Ending:**
  - Dad crawls under the blankie to hold it. That is how dads sleep.
  - 20 years later he tucks it, unwashed, over his newborn.
  - Last line: "Last night, it moved."
- **Roblox visuals:** a washing-machine set piece and a parade of cute-creepy nightmare creatures.
- **Risks:** many creatures make it costly to animate, and the emotion is thinner.

### 16. Count the Thunder (partly TF, 57/80)
- **Rule (frame 1):** "In this world, thunder isn't weather. It's footsteps." *Frame 1:* lightning over a field, a giant silhouette inside the cloud, BOOM.
- **Secret:** storm giants walk the sky. The seconds between flash and boom tell you how far away the nearest one is. They have been searching for 300 years.
- **Society:**
  - Lightning rods sold as "giant repellent".
  - Storm-cellar drills, with kids counting aloud in class.
  - The hill outside town, shaped like a sleeping child, is called Sleeping Hill.
- **Lock / key:** giants can't see in the dark / they can see a light that's left on.
- **Person + plant:** a girl of 11, terrified of storms. Grandpa's three rules: count out loud, never hide under a tree, leave a light on so they know we're friendly.
- **Twist 1 (~0:30):** the count goes 5, 3, 1, 0. A giant kneels over the house, its eye at her window.
- **Middle:** it isn't angry. It is searching, and it reaches toward Sleeping Hill.
- **Twist 2 (~1:25):** the hill is their child, asleep for 300 years, and the town is built on its blanket.
- **Ending:**
  - She carries Grandpa's lantern up the hill in the storm.
  - The hill stands up and walks home.
  - 20 years later, in the first storm since, the count stops at "one Mississippi". It came back to visit.
- **Roblox visuals:** huge storm silhouettes, lightning, the hill rising. Spectacular, but a heavy build.
- **Risks:** the hardest idea to animate, and the lowest emotional payoff.
