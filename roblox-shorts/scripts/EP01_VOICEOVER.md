# Never Move Grandpa: voice-over text

Read this for the recording. The full script with timings, picture notes and the backup script is in EP01_FINAL.md.


### Full narration (read top to bottom in one take; CAPS = stress)

Paragraph break = take a breath. "..." = a real pause of about 0.8 s (there are 4). Quoted lines are in the character's voice; the next block lists them.

```
In this world, grandparents don't die, they turn into garden GNOMES.

First, your grandma gets SHORTER. One morning, a gnome is wearing her GLASSES. Nobody knows why they face the HOUSE.

Houses here come with ANCESTORS. You never step on a lawn after DARK. Next door, the gnomes are PLASTIC. Their dog went down a HOLE.

Your gnome can't walk out of its GARDEN. Only FAMILY can carry it out.

MY grandpa turned last spring. I say goodnight, and he BLINKS. For 60 years, he walked our lawn at nine. Flashlight, slippers, one LAP.

"Checking for moles," he'd say. "Big ones."

Then, the night before we moved, at nine, his spot was EMPTY...

So I ran out barefoot, after DARK. He was at the GATE, facing the moving truck. He was trying to come WITH me.

"Come on, then." I picked him up. Only family can.

Then the lawn MOVED. Dozens of mounds, with eyes like bike REFLECTORS. Moles, BIG ones.

Grandpa's lantern lit, and they stopped at its EDGE. Down the street, every lantern lit, except NEXT DOOR. Gnomes aren't decorations. They're the FENCE...

The new family's boy, Theo, six, was already asleep in my old ROOM. Take Grandpa, and Theo's window goes DARK. Leave him, and I never get another BLINK. And the truck left at DAWN.

So I stood Mom's plastic gnome under Theo's window. GULP. Gone.

The mounds rolled closer, to the flowers, to the bricks, to the GLASS. So I ran back, and they backed OFF. Fine, I'd stand guard till dawn. And tomorrow NIGHT?

Grandpa turned his head, away from me, toward my old WINDOW.

Flashlight, slippers, one lap. And one stop, always under my WINDOW. Checking for moles? He was checking on ME.

Before me, he checked on DAD. For 60 years, whoever was small in that ROOM. That's why they face the HOUSE. I wasn't small anymore. THEO was.

I knelt in the flowerbed...

I said goodnight, and he blinked slowly, like he was SORRY. I set him down, and I let GO.

FWOOM. His lantern flared, light rolled across the lawn, and every mound sank, eyes winking out pair by PAIR. Then he stood still, facing Theo's WINDOW.

At dawn, Theo came out in his socks. "Is he NICE?" "The nicest. Say goodnight, and he'll BLINK." Theo tried it, and Grandpa BLINKED. At THEO.

From the truck, every gnome faced its house, except ONE.

... Every spring for 60 years, Theo's sent me a PHOTO. Grandpa, same flowerbed, same window, always mid-BLINK.

I'm 71, and my granddaughter says I'm getting SHORTER. I've already picked my WINDOW.
```

### Reading guide

- **Length:** 435 words (436 spoken). Target **2:21 at 190 wpm**: an excited kid telling a story, not an announcer. Acceptable range 2:14-2:28 (200-180 wpm).
- **Timed read first.** Read the block once with a stopwatch before recording.
  - Over 2:28: speed up 1.05x in the edit (never above 1.10x), or cut words from beats 3, 7 or 9 only. Never cut from beats 1, 6, 10 or 12, and never cut the 4 pauses.
  - **Long version:** if the master reads in **2:21 or less**, add S1 after line 37 (about 2:24 at 190 wpm). If it reads in **2:18 or less**, add S2 as well (about 2:24 at 195 wpm). Both lines are in beat 9, where the formula puts the 2:30 stretch.
  - Don't stop on every `/`. If each of the 19 lifts becomes a 0.3 s stop, the read grows by about 6 s (2:34 at 180 wpm).
- **Voices**
  - Narrator, lines 1-61: an 11-year-old boy telling it straight. Use your own voice, lifted, brighter and a little quicker. No adult tells.
  - Narrator, lines 62-65: the same boy at 71. About 10% slower, lower, warm, a smile in it. This is the reveal that the kid was an old man all along, so don't age the voice earlier.
  - GRANDPA (15, 17): warm, gravelly, slower, a wink in it.
  - THEO (57): small, sleepy, pitched up.
  - ME (22, 58): the hero's own voice, whispered.
- **Slow down:** line 18 (the twist), 21, 30 (FENCE), the whole of twist 2 (42-49, slower with each line), the choice (50-55), and the ending (62-65).
- **Speed up:** 19 (running out barefoot), 25-27 (the lawn moves), 35-39 (the attempt, rising).
- **Whisper:** "his spot was EMPTY" (18), "Come on, then." (22), "I said goodnight… like he was SORRY" (51), "and I let GO" (52), "The nicest…" (58).
- **Stress:** the CAPS words, at most one per sentence. Lean on them by lengthening and lifting the word, not by shouting. The ten that carry the story: GNOMES (01), HOUSE (04, and line 48 said the same way), EMPTY (18), WITH (21), BIG (27), FENCE (30), ME (45), THEO (49), GO (52), WINDOW (65).
- **Rehearse these:**
  - "The new family's boy, Theo, six," (line 31) is a three-beat list. Don't rush it.
  - "Checking for moles?" (45) is a choked half-laugh. It quotes Grandpa back to himself.
  - "eyes winking out pair by PAIR" (54) is the slowest phrase in the long sentence.
  - In "Theo's sent me a PHOTO" (62), "Theo's" means "Theo has".
