## 1. Title, rule, synopsis

**Title: Never Move Grandpa**

A forbidden act. It names the premise and the plot's hinge (the move) but not the twist.

**4 alternatives:**

| # | Title | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **Grandpa Is A Gnome** | The rule as a statement and the most clickable for kids. Use it as the B test against the main title |
| 3 | Keep Off The Lawn | The in-world sign. Risk: it reads like a grumpy-neighbour meme |
| 4 | Checking For Moles | Means nothing before watching and everything after. Good for a re-upload or a pinned comment |
| 5 | Why Gnomes Face Houses | The spine question from 0:07. The answer comes at 1:38 |

**The one-sentence rule:** In this world, grandparents don't die, they turn into garden gnomes.

**Synopsis:**
The night before his family moves out, an 11-year-old finds his grandpa, now a garden gnome, at the gate trying to follow him. Then the lawn rises with giant star-nosed moles that only the gnomes' lanterns hold back.
He learns that Grandpa's nightly "mole checks" were 60 years of guarding whoever slept in his room, so he leaves Grandpa to guard the new boy, Theo. Sixty years later he is 71, getting shorter, and has already picked his window.

**Thumbnail:** the gnome at the gate, boots on the shimmering property line, one dewdrop tear, the moving truck's open back behind him. No text, or the title in 2-3 big words.

**How to read this page**
- Each line shows its number, its estimated start time at **190 wpm** (pauses included), its beat, the speaker, the text exactly as it is to be read, a delivery note in brackets, and a picture note.
- **CAPS** = the stress word, at most one per sentence. The caption pops and the cut lands on it.
- `/` = a short lift (about 0.3 s at most). Don't stop dead on it. There are 19.
- `//` = a planned pause of about 0.8 s. There are 4: after EMPTY, after FENCE, after "flowerbed", and before "Every spring". Keep them in the edit.
- A speaker other than NARRATOR is a quoted line the narrator acts: GRANDPA, THEO, or ME (the hero speaking in the scene).
- S1 and S2 are optional stretch lines. They are not in the timings or the word count. Section 3 says when to use them.

### Build sheet

**Cast**
- **The hero and narrator (11):** a blocky Roblox boy in a mustard hoodie, with messy brown hair and a freckles decal. He is barefoot from line 19. At 71 (lines 62-65, and secretly in frame 1) he has grey hair and a grey moustache, a mustard cardigan and an olive fishing hat like Grandpa's.
- **Grandpa, human (flashbacks only):** tall, olive fishing hat with lures, white walrus moustache, brown cardigan, tartan slippers, a flashlight.
- **Grandpa, gnome:** knee-high ceramic, the same hat and moustache, a **red** coat. He holds a small brass lantern that stays **unlit until line 28**. His painted eyes blink with a two-frame decal swap (a slow four-frame blink at line 51).
- **The frame-1 gnome:** the same build and hat, but a **mustard** coat. It is the narrator's own gnome, a clue for rewatchers.
- **Theo (6):** oversized blue pyjamas, always carrying a grey-blue stuffed whale. In the photos at 66 he is bald with a grey beard.
- **The moles:** giant star-nosed moles. They travel as rolling turf mounds. Only a pink star-shaped snout (dinner-plate size, soft wriggling tentacles) and two round amber "bike reflector" eyes (neon material) break the surface. No teeth, no mouth on show. They sniff, wriggle and recoil from lantern light: cute-creepy, not gory.
- **Street gnomes:** assorted, each with its own small brass lantern, all facing their houses. Next door's are glossy plastic with the price stickers still on.
- **Mom, Dad, the new family, the granddaughter:** background only, no lines.

**Sets**
1. Our front garden: a bungalow, the hero's bedroom window at gnome height, the flowerbed under it (Grandpa's spot), the lawn, the gate and the kerb.
2. The street of front gardens, at dusk, at night and at dawn.
3. Next door's lawn, with the kennel.
4. The flashback lawn: sepia for the hero's childhood, black and white for Dad's.
5. The epilogue: his daughter's back garden at dusk, with a striped deck chair (the same model as frame 1) facing the granddaughter's lit window.

**Colour arc**
- Frame 1: dusk orange. The world montage (0:03-0:31) mixes sunrise, day and dusk.
- **Night, 0:34-1:58: high-key.** Lavender and pink sky, soft blue shadows, lantern gold as the only warm accent. Never crush it to dark navy: 85 s of dark blue dies on a phone screen.
- Pink dawn, 1:58-2:10.
- Epilogue dusk, graded to match frame 1 so the loop is seamless.

**SFX hit points:** dandelion poof (01); blink "tik" (12, 51 slowed, 59); low rumble (25); lantern "tink-whoosh" (28) and a cascade of tinks down the street (29); GULP (36); stony creak (42); FWOOM (53); truck engine (60).
