## How to read this page

- **CAPS** = the stress word. The caption and the cut land on it.
- `/` = a short breath (0.3 s).
- `//` = a planned pause (0.6-1.0 s). There are 4. Keep them in the edit.
- The narrator voices every line. Quoted lines are acted by him and tagged:
  - **[RADIO]** is the town loudspeaker: flat, crackly announcer voice. Caption colour yellow.
  - **[MOM]** is softer and a little higher. Caption colour pink.
  - **[ME]** is the narrator quoting himself as an adult, in his normal voice.
  - The wind creature never speaks. It talks only with gestures, like a silent companion.
- The voice is a kid telling the story, in present tense up to 0:28 and past tense from "So at 3:15". At 2:02 we learn he is 30. There are no adult tells before that.
- Captions show numbers as digits: 3:15, 1987, 47, 0, 1 MINUTE, 7, 3, 20, 3:14.
- The time before each line is its start time at 195 wpm, including the planned pauses.

## World rules (keep every shot consistent with these)

1. If you pull a face when the wind changes, your face sticks forever (*click*). Your real face blows away to the church steeple.
2. The wind is a creature: whirling leaves around the church's rusty rooster weathervane, with a blank oval for a face. It keeps the real faces on the steeple walls and tries them on.
3. **Lock:** it never gives a face back. **Key:** it only trades, taking a real face given on purpose in exchange for someone else's.
4. It takes one face per person, ever. It took the boy's at 7, so nothing sticks on him on the hill, and it refuses all his silly faces. The only face of his it will take is that same one, back.
5. A wind change lasts one minute. The rooster on the creature's head swings round and home again like a clock hand.
6. **The secret.** At 7, the boy cried outside at Grandpa's funeral and his face stuck. Mom traded her real face (her laugh) for his, and his crying face moved onto her. The town thinks she cried at the funeral, and she told him he slept through it.
7. **Visual-only plant: three freckles on the left cheek.** The boy has them, and so does Mom's crying face. Mom's real face (the fridge photo, the steeple wall) does not.
