# Head-writer critique: write-1 "Don't Pull That Face" vs write-2 "Keep Off The Lawn"

Adversarial review of `scripts/draft_write-1.md` (Wind Change) and `scripts/draft_write-2.md` (Grandpa Gnome) against `docs/SCRIPT_FORMULA.md`, `docs/NICHE_NOTES.md` and the five reference analyses. The bar is a channel that needs millions of views per short.

**How I measured.**
- I counted words, sentences and openers again from each draft's read-aloud copy. I did not use the drafts' own audits.
- I timed every line at 180, 190 and 195 wpm, with each `//` pause counted as 0.8 s.
- Each draft has about 41-44 `/` breath marks. I timed them both ways:
  - inside the wpm, which is how the drafts timed them;
  - as real 0.3 s pauses, which is what a first-time narrator does when he obeys the marks.
- Working files (counter, timer, both revised copies) are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-claude-todo-demo/353ac1d9-152b-55bb-bdf1-0625e0fc6a83/scratchpad/rbx/work/critique/`.

## Verdict

**The winner is write-2, "Grandpa Gnome": 111/150 against 106/150.** It wins on what a Short is punished for first:
- the first 3 seconds;
- whether a 10-year-old can follow it on one listen;
- a loop that sends viewers straight back to frame 1.

Its problems cost few words to fix. With the fixes in section 5, the master is **435 words: 2:17 at 195 wpm and 2:28 at 180 wpm**. Twist 1 lands at **0:33 / 0:36**.

**Write-1 is the runner-up, with the higher ceiling.** Its twist 2 is the best beat in either script ("Nobody's that BRAVE. / Mom WAS."). As written, though, the rules about trading faces cannot be followed on one listen, the hero's plan contradicts the world, and the read runs **2:33 at 180 wpm**.

Make write-1 the second short, after the must-fix pass in section 6. That pass is measured at 429 words: 2:18 at 195 wpm and 2:29 at 180 wpm.

**Nobody has done these yet:**
- the YouTube Shorts and TikTok search for either rule phrase;
- a timed read by the owner.

Both must happen before production (section 7).

### 5.2 Structural fixes

1. **Make the narrator a boy, the grandson.** No spoken word changes.
   - The owner can then voice the 11-year-old and the 71-year-old man himself.
   - In the epilogue, the old narrator wears Grandpa's olive fishing hat in the striped deck chair. The last shot then *is* frame 1, so on replay the opening "grandparents turn into gnomes" shows the narrator himself turning. This is the biggest loop upgrade available.
2. **Give the threat a face.** Make the mounds giant star-nosed moles: only the pink star-shaped snout and the bike-reflector eyes break the turf, with no teeth.
   - It pays off "Checking for moles. Big ones." literally.
   - It gives the short the cute-creepy creature every reference has.
   - It adds a thumbnail insert.
3. **Why Grandpa stays, shown in the picture with no extra words.**
   - At 0:31, the SOLD sign reads "COMES WITH ANCESTORS", and a BALCONY GNOME box sits on the truck (a flat has no garden).
   - At 1:10, Mom's plastic gnome comes out of that box.
   - Only if the timed read leaves room, say "the night before we moved to the city" (+3 words; "EMPTY" then moves to about 0:36.7 at 180 wpm).
4. **Grade the night high-key**, lavender and pink like r4, with lantern gold as the accent. 85 s of dark blue will crush on a phone.
5. **Read marks.**
   - Cut the `/` breaths from 41 to 20 or fewer: keep them only before a punch word or in lines over 8 words.
   - Use at most one CAPS word per sentence.
   - Keep the four `//` pauses: after EMPTY, after FENCE, after "flowerbed", and before "Every spring".
   - The revised copy below already follows this.
6. **Thumbnail:** the gnome at the gate, boots on the shimmering line, a dewdrop tear, the truck behind. Emotion beats the lantern street at thumbnail size.
7. **Title:** use "**Never Move Grandpa**" as option A: a forbidden act that names the premise and the plot's hinge. Use "Grandpa Is A Gnome" as B. "Keep Off The Lawn" reads as a grumpy-neighbour meme, with no stakes and the hook hidden.
8. **Lock and key (optional).** The key pays at 0:43, not at the climax. If the owner wants the climax to hinge on the rule, plant a third clause at 0:20: "Carry it out, and it can never come BACK." (+7 words, so cut R13 and keep S1 out). The hero's one step toward the gate at 1:03 then becomes final, not just costly.
